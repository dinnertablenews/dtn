At age 7: The country had a big vote on Tuesday, and the president's team lost a lot of seats. His helper, the vice president, said the reason was the war with Iran. Lots of people didn't want it, and gas cost more. Like a coach saying out loud which play lost the game. The grown-ups are sorting it out.

At 9: In February the US and Israel attacked Iran. Gas got expensive, and on Tuesday many 2024 Trump voters picked Democrats. Vance wrote that chain out: the war cost the election. Trump agreed, then said he'd do it again. An election is a report card every two years. This one got read out loud.

At 17: Vance never named Trump, but wrote that anyone who thinks the war was worth losing the Senate for six years should say so. Honesty or ambition? Second: Trump says the war "largely" lost the election and he'd do it again, because Iran can't have a bomb. Can a leader be right and still deserve to lose?

Why this works at age 17: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the vice president broke with the president in public over the war, and the president called the post fine and says he would do it again. The script asks whether honesty and ambition can be one move. Expect "so who's right?" Say what you think. They're testing.

The dinner table question: If your team loses, should the captain say whose fault it was? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
