He wrote that he did. Three times.

The Vatican published excerpts on Tuesday. Francis wrote in a leather-bound calendar from 1992, when he was a bishop in Buenos Aires, to August 2024. He describes seeing the devil three times and a crisis in 2021-22 over his urge to run everything alone. Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández, who edited it, says Francis wanted it published.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: Pope Francis, who led the Catholic Church, kept a diary for 32 years. He wanted people to read it, and now it is a book. A diary is like a box where you keep your thoughts, even the scary ones. He wrote his scary ones down, and he got through them. The grown-ups have it.

At 12: Pope Francis kept a diary from 1992 to 2024, and now it is a book. He describes seeing the devil three times, and two years when he felt he had to run everything alone. A diary works like a coach's notebook: write the mistakes down so you can see them. His last two books came out of that stretch.

At 15: Should a private diary be published after the writer dies? Francis kept his for 32 years and wanted it out, his editor says. He writes of shame and two years of feeling he had to run everything alone. Second: in 2018 he dismissed abuse victims in Chile, then wrote of "the entrance to hell." Does that count?

Why this works at age 12: At 12 a child is working out cause and effect, and this story is a chain they can hold: a bad stretch, written down for two years, then two books that came out of it. The coach's notebook is the mechanism, mistakes on paper so you can see them. Expect 'did he really see the devil?' He wrote that he did, three times. The rest is theirs.
