Over the internet. The chip stays put; the work travels.

The US already bans selling its most advanced AI chips to China. Chinese companies rented them instead, from data centers in places like Malaysia. On Monday the Commerce Department closed that door: data centers serving China, Russia, Iran or Cuba must now name their customers, and chip sales to five countries need a license.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: America makes the best computer chips for teaching computers to think, and it won't sell those to China. So some companies in China used them over the internet instead, like bouncing on a neighbour's trampoline instead of buying one. Today America said that has to stop too. It's a rule about machines, and the grown-ups have it.

At 12: The US won't sell its best AI chips to China. But a chip in a Malaysian data center can be rented over the internet, so the ban had a hole. On Monday the Commerce Department patched it: data centers serving China must name their customers. A locked door is no use with the window open.

At 17: Can a country keep its technology to itself once the technology lives in the cloud? China can't buy US chips, so its AI firms rented them from Malaysia. Monday's rule says that counts too. Second: it came days after Nvidia's CEO quit over ties to a firm suspected of just this. A fix, or a headline?

Why this works at age 12: At twelve a child is tracing cause and effect and checking whether adults apply a rule all the way through. The script gives the chain, a ban, a hole, a patch, and the open window makes the hole visible. Expect "why didn't they close it before?" A congressman proposed it last year. The bill has passed one chamber.
