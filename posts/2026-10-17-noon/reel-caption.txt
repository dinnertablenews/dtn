Everyone is short. The US isn't even in the treaty.

In 2022, nearly 200 countries agreed in Montreal to protect 30% of the world's land and water by 2030, and to raise $200 billion a year to pay for it. They are raising $75 to $92 billion. A UN report out Thursday puts the land on track for 26%, the sea for 12%. Talks open Monday in Yerevan, Armenia. The US is not in the treaty.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: Almost 200 countries made a promise to keep nature safe: about a third of the land and the sea, for the animals and the trees. Picture a big jar with a line near the top, and every country dropping coins in. Right now the jar is less than half full. Next week the grown-ups meet in a city called Yerevan to work on filling it.

At 10: Nearly 200 countries signed a checklist for nature in 2022: protect 30% of land and sea by 2030, raise $200 billion a year to pay for it. The UN checked the list Thursday. Fewer than half the targets are on track, and the money is under half the goal. Monday they meet in Armenia.

At 17: If 200 countries promise $200 billion a year and bring in under half, is that failure or progress? The UN says 26% of land and 12% of sea by 2030, against 30%. Second: the US isn't in the treaty at all. Does staying out make it blameless, or just absent?

Why this works at age 17: A teenager can hold two true things at once: $75 to $92 billion a year is a lot of money, and less than half the promise. Kuhn's finding is that argument is practised against someone honest, so the script leaves the US question open. Expect "why isn't the US in it?" The article doesn't say. Don't guess.
