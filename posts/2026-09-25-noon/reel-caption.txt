At age 7: Two years ago a man fired a gun at a big outdoor rally where Trump was speaking. A man named Jim in the stands was hit and never got all the way better. He died this week. He was 76, about as old as a great-grandpa. That was far from here, and the man with the gun was stopped that same day. You're safe.

At 12: In 2024 a gunman on a rooftop fired at a Trump rally. Jim Copenhaver was hit twice; a bullet stayed near his spine. He died Wednesday. Security is a checklist: who's on the roof, who watches the crowd. His family says a step was skipped.

At 17: If a man dies two years after being shot, did the gunman kill him? Pennsylvania's attorney general says the wounds did. No court will say: the gunman died that day. Second: Copenhaver sued the Secret Service in June. When protection fails, who owes what?

Why this works at age 7: A seven-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script keeps the gun two years back and stopped, and puts a man who never got all the way better in front. Great-grandpa's age is the anchor. Expect "could that happen to you?" No. That was one rally, far from here, and it is over.
