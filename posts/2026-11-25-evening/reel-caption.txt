Split verdict. Guilty of theft and fraud, not racketeering.

A New York jury convicted Huawei on Tuesday of conspiring to steal trade secrets and of bank fraud, and acquitted it of racketeering, the top charge. Prosecutors said the Chinese telecom giant sold routers with Cisco's code inside and photographed a rival's device at a trade show. Huawei calls the acquittal a vindication and will appeal.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: A big company from China that makes phones got in trouble in a New York court. A jury said it copied other companies' secret work, like copying a friend's drawing and saying you made it. The company says it didn't and will ask another court to look again. It isn't allowed to sell to American phone networks anyway, so nothing changes for us.

At 12: Prosecutors said Huawei spent years taking rivals' secrets: it sold routers with Cisco's code inside, and a worker photographed the inside of a rival's device at a trade show. The jury, the referee here, called two fouls and waved off the biggest one, racketeering. Huawei says the no is what matters.

At 13: Can a company commit a crime, or only the people in it? Huawei's lawyer said a few of its 200,000 employees did wrong and the firm fixed it; the prosecutor said the tone was set at the top. Second: the US barred Huawei from its networks years before this verdict. So what was the trial for?

Why this works at age 12: A child this age is working out whether rules apply to everyone, and this is a referee calling two fouls and waving off the third. The script hands over the chain, copied code to trade-show photo to the 2019 charges to Tuesday, and a split verdict both sides claim. Expect 'so who won?' Both say they did. That is a split decision.
