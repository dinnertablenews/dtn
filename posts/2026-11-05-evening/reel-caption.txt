Russell Brand, 51, was convicted on Thursday of rape and sexual assault. The allegations date back more than 20 years. Brand denied the charges and testified that he was obsessed with sex as a young man but never forced anyone. The judge said he will get a prison term. At age 6: A famous man named Russell Brand went to court. A court is a big room where a judge sits up front, like a teacher's desk, and listens to both sides before deciding. They decided he hurt some people a long time ago, and that is what guilty means. He's going to prison, and the grown-ups have it.

At 9: Russell Brand was found guilty of rape and sexual assault from more than 20 years ago. People reported him, police investigated, a court heard both sides. He said he never forced anyone; it believed the accusers, like a referee who checks the replay and keeps the call.

At 14: Brand testified he was obsessed with sex as a young man but never forced anyone. Why doesn't that defence work? The question was consent, not how much sex he had. Second: the allegations are more than 20 years old. Should there be a time limit on reporting?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so court is a room with a desk and a grown-up who listens to both sides. What Brand did stays as 'hurt people': this age takes one vivid feature for the whole event, and the feature should be that a grown-up decided. Expect "did he say sorry?" No. He says he didn't do it.
