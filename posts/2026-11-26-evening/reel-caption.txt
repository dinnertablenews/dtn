The moon and sun pulling together. The year's highest water.

King tides, the highest of the year, reached California this week. On Wednesday a wave tore part of the pier in Pacifica, south of San Francisco, into the ocean. Beachfront homes flooded. Waves of 20 to 30 feet were forecast for the Bay Area. San Diego's tide hit 8 feet 11 inches, likely a record, with a super El Niño pushing it higher.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: The ocean in California came up very high this week, the highest it gets all year. One big wave knocked a piece of a long pier into the water, and some houses by the beach got wet inside. The waves were as tall as a two-story house. The pier was already closed, and the grown-ups are keeping people off the beach.

At 10: The moon and the sun both pull on the ocean. A few times a year they line up, and the water rises higher than any other day: a king tide. This year a super El Niño warmed the Pacific, and warm water takes up more room. Think of a bathtub already full when someone climbs in.

At 14: Who pays when the ocean takes a pier? Pacifica closed its pier in summer for cracks; a king tide finished it on Wednesday. San Diego broke a tide record set last month, and the BBC says king tides come more often as seas rise. Second: who decides when a beach house is too close?

Why this works at age 10: A child this age builds chains of cause and effect. This one is complete: two pulls line up, the water rises, a warm ocean takes up more room, so the year's highest tide came in higher still and took a pier with it. The bathtub makes the extra volume real. Expect 'will it happen again?' Yes, and more often as seas rise.
