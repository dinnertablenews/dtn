It can't be moved. A bump could set it off.

The Army plans to detonate 68 barrels of lead azide, 9,500 pounds, at its ammunition plant in Middletown, Iowa, on Wednesday. Staff found the barrels in March, crystallised and too unstable to move. The dust could carry lead 100 miles. Trump paused it Monday; then an appeals court lifted its stay and the Army said it is going ahead.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: In Iowa, the Army has 68 old barrels of explosive that got too touchy to move, so on Wednesday they will set it off on purpose, with everyone far away. It weighs about as much as three cars. People nearby will stay inside with the windows shut until the dust settles. The grown-ups have been planning it since March.

At 8: In old barrels, lead azide grew crystals so touchy a bump could set them off, and cold makes it worse, so the Army blows up all 68 barrels Wednesday, before it freezes. The dust carries lead, a poison, so people nearby stay in and wash up after. Like ripping off a bandage: once, on your terms.

At 15: Why blow it up instead of moving it? The Army says a bump could set off 9,500 pounds of crystallised lead azide, so a planned blast, dust and all, beats a surprise one. Second: Trump passed the call to a court and the governor-elect, who passed it back. Who owns it?

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can hold two true things at once, and this story is two: a controlled blast spreads lead, and an uncontrolled one could come any day. The second pair is a president and a governor-elect each saying the other should decide. Give them both sides and let them argue. Expect 'who's right?' Nobody has shown a safe option.

The dinner table question: If every option is risky, who should get to decide? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
