Once counted, a ballot can't be traced back to the voter.

The Justice Department says about 20,000 California voters may not be citizens and wants their ballots held back until it checks. On Friday a 9th Circuit panel said no, 2-1: there is no workable way to pull those ballots without wrecking the count. A district judge said the same Wednesday. The DOJ says it will go to the Supreme Court.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: Grown-ups in California are voting right now, mostly by mail. Washington said some ballots might belong to people who aren't allowed to vote, and asked to pull them out. A court said no. They're mixed in now, like sugar stirred into tea, and nobody's vote gets lost.

At 10: The DOJ has a list of about 20,000 California voters it says may not be citizens, and asked a court to hold their ballots back. California says the list is full of errors. Two judges of three said no: once a ballot is counted, nobody can tell whose it was. Like pulling five tests from a graded, shuffled stack.

At 14: If a list of 20,000 suspected noncitizen voters might be mostly wrong, who carries the risk: the state, or the people on it? The DOJ says every illegal ballot cancels a citizen's vote. The 9th Circuit said, 2-1, that pulling those ballots would wreck the count. Which mistake is worse?

Why this works at age 10: At this age a child is working out cause and effect, so the script runs the chain from list to request to ruling, and the shuffled stack shows why the court could not say yes. Expect "What if some really weren't citizens?" Say that is the dissenting judge's point, and the other two decided wrecking the count was the bigger harm.

The dinner table question: Which is worse: one wrong vote counted, or one right vote lost? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
