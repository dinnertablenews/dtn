Yes, it can be done. It became a union's rallying cry.

Dolores Huerta died on Wednesday at her home in Bakersfield, California. She was 96. She was a teacher in the San Joaquin Valley who quit because her students came to class hungry, and went to organize farm workers with César Chavez. Their union became United Farm Workers. She was jailed 22 times and got the Medal of Freedom in 2012.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: A woman named Dolores Huerta died on Wednesday. She was 96, as old as a great-great-grandmother. She spent her whole life helping the people who pick our fruit and vegetables get treated fairly, and her saying was "Sí, se puede," which means "Yes, it can be done." She lived a long, full life, and the grown-ups have it.

At 10: Dolores Huerta was a teacher whose students came to class hungry. She quit to organize the people picking the crops, with César Chavez, and their union joined a grape strike that ran five years. A union is the whole class refusing to play until the rule is fair: one picker can be replaced, hundreds can't.

At 17: Would you keep a secret for decades to protect your life's work? Dolores Huerta built the United Farm Workers beside César Chavez. This year she said he raped her in those early years, and she stayed quiet for the movement. Second: does "Sí, se puede" belong to her, or to him?

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out cause and effect and whether rules are fair, so the script runs her life as one chain: hungry students, a union, a five-year strike. The class refusing to play says why one picker alone could not have done it. Expect "did they win?" Yes. Contracts with the big growers won benefits for the fields.

The dinner table question: Would you keep a secret for decades to protect something you built? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
