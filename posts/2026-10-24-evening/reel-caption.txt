At age 6: You might hear that a man fell off a roller coaster in Georgia and died. The park is putting seat belts on that ride and the ones like it, the way a car has a belt as well as a locked door. The ride stays shut while they check it. The grown-ups have it.

At 8: On October 12 a man fell from Goliath, a 200-foot coaster in Georgia, and died. The ride holds you with a padded bar over your lap. Now Six Flags will add seat belts to Goliath and every coaster with that bar, and nine stay shut for now. A belt under a bar is a second lock on the same door.

At 15: Who decides a ride is safe, the park or the state? After a man was thrown from Goliath, Six Flags is belting every coaster with that lap bar, and nine stay shut. SeaWorld has the same bar on Mako and reopened it in a week. Same bar, two answers. Which would you trust?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid detail for the whole story, and the one on offer is a man in the air, so the script hands them another: the seat belt they click every morning. Cantor found this age fears what it can picture. Expect "can I still go on rides?" Yes, with the bar and the belt.
