SpaceX's Starship, the biggest rocket ever built, reached orbit for the first time Monday from Starbase, Texas. One engine shut down early, and controllers waited several tense minutes before pressing on. It released 26 Starlink satellites and was to circle Earth six times. At age 6: A rocket called Starship flew into space today and went around the Earth for the first time. It is 407 feet tall, longer than a football field stood on its end. Nobody was inside, just 26 small satellites that popped out one by one. One engine stopped early, but the rest kept going, and the grown-ups in the control room had it.

At 10: Starship flew 13 times in three years and never got past the Indian Ocean, often in flames. Today an engine quit on the way up. The rest covered, so controllers checked the numbers and let it go on to orbit. A rowing crew with one oar down: the rest pull harder.

At 13: Musk says SpaceX won't fly Starship over land yet, because if debris fell on people "our popularity would diminish very rapidly." Safety or PR, and does it matter? Second: NASA needs Starship for Artemis III next summer. Should a moon program ride on one company's tests?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script gives them the height and the empty seats. The engine that quit stays in, because the rest kept going and the control room decided, which is the reassurance. Expect "will people ride it?" Not yet. NASA wants it for the moon, and the article says that is years off.
