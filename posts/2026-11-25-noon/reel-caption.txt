Pakistan has no rocket. China does, and invited him.

Shenzhou-24 lifted off from the Gobi desert on Wednesday with three astronauts for China's Tiangong space station. One is Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old Pakistani fighter pilot and the first foreigner on a Chinese crewed flight. Pakistan has no spacecraft. He will test seeds and bacteria in orbit, then come home after a few days.

Source: BBC News

At age 6: A rocket took off from a desert in China this morning with three people inside. One of them is the first astronaut Pakistan has ever had, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, who used to fly fighter jets. A few minutes after liftoff, about as long as brushing your teeth, it was declared a success. He will float up there a few days, test some seeds, and come home.

At 10: Pakistan has no spacecraft, so it cannot send anyone to space on its own. China has a space station, Tiangong, and wants other countries to use it, so it trained two Pakistani pilots and gave one a seat. It works like a carpool: the family with the car decides who rides.

At 16: Is a ride to space a gift or a message? China trained Pakistan's pilots, flew one to Tiangong, and said it hopes the flight strengthens ties with Islamabad. Second: Ali said he carries "the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis." Does it matter whose rocket got him there?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script gives them one person, one rocket and a desert they can picture. Minutes are too abstract on their own; brushing teeth is a span they know. Expect 'can I go?' Not yet. Pakistan waited until this morning, and Ali trained in China and had to learn Chinese first.
