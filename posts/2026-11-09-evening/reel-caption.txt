Pope Leo XIV began his first full day in Argentina on Monday at the Cottolengo Don Orione, a home south of Buenos Aires for more than 400 people with disabilities. At age 6: The pope is visiting Argentina, a country at the bottom of South America. On Monday he went first to a home where 400 people with disabilities live, about as many as a whole school. He hugged people in wheelchairs and told them their lives are a blessing. Some cried happy tears, then millions came to pray with him.

At 8: Argentina's president, Javier Milei, cut spending hard, and payments for people with disabilities froze. Congress passed a law to fund them over his veto; he still hasn't paid. On Monday the pope made a home for 400 of them his first stop. Like a referee pointing at a rule the players stopped following.

At 16: If a law passes over your veto, can you just not pay? Argentina's Congress voted more money for disability care over Milei's veto; his government hasn't paid, and the home the pope visited Monday is still owed. Second: he never named Milei. Does a rebuke count if you skip the name?

Why this works at age 8: Between eight and twelve a child is testing whether adults follow their own rules, so the script hands them a chain: a law passed over a veto, money still unpaid, a visitor pointing at it. The referee is the mechanism. Expect 'can he get in trouble for not paying?' Say the report doesn't say, and that the Senate votes on the money soon.
