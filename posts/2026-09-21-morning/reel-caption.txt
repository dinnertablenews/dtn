President Trump said Sunday that the arch he wants between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery will double as a military complex: drones and snipers on the roof and plaza, ammunition in storage. He says the military asked. The Pentagon said it had nothing beyond his statement. A lawsuit says Congress must approve it.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: The president wants to build a huge arch near Washington, half as tall as the Washington Monument. On Sunday he said soldiers would keep watch from the top. It isn't built yet, and lots of grown-ups still have to say yes. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: Trump wants a 250-foot arch at the bridge into Washington. On Sunday he said it will hold drones and snipers too, because the military asked. The Pentagon had nothing to add. Think of a permission slip: a commission still has to vote, and a lawsuit says Congress must sign too.

At 13: What do you make of it when the president says the military asked for something and the Pentagon has nothing to add? That's the arch: snipers and drones, he says, at the military's request. If a building is called national security, should it need fewer approvals, or more?

Why this works at age 13: A teenager can hold two true things at once and reason about what is not in front of them, and this story is that: a claim from the president, silence from the Pentagon, a label now on two buildings. The script hands them the gap, not a verdict. Expect “so is he lying?” Say what is known, then ask what would count as evidence.

The dinner table question: Should a monument do a second job, or just stand there? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
