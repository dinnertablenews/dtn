Twelve states sued in July to block Paramount's $81 billion purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery. On Monday they settled. At age 7: The company that makes Top Gun is buying the company that makes Harry Potter. Twelve states said hold on, that's a lot for one company to own, and took it to court. On Monday they agreed: the deal can happen if Paramount makes at least 30 movies a year. It's like two big toy boxes tipped into one, with a grown-up counting what goes in.

At 10: Paramount is buying Warner Bros.: one owner for CBS, CNN, HBO Max and Harry Potter. Twelve states sued in July. On Monday they settled: 30 films a year, money for laid-off workers, a monitor to check Paramount keeps its word. A teacher letting two friends sit together, on conditions, and checking.

At 13: If you think two companies shouldn't merge, do you settle anyway? Rob Bonta did Monday: he still says the two shouldn't combine, but he traded a trial for 30 films a year and a monitor on CBS and CNN's news. Did he win, or fold?

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out cause and effect and whether the rules get applied, and the settlement is a chain they can follow: a merger, a lawsuit, promises, a monitor to check them. The teacher who sets conditions and checks back is the mechanism. Expect "why did the states give up?" Bonta still says the two should not merge. He took promises he can enforce instead of a trial.
