A committee in Sweden votes. This year it picked her.

Canadian poet Anne Carson, 76, won the Nobel Prize in Literature on Thursday. The Swedish Academy cited a "bold and inventive" body of work that plays with the Greek classics and makes new forms from them. She taught classics for over 30 years. The call found her in Iceland, "listening to the wind." It pays 12 million kronor, about $1.2m.

Source: BBC News

At age 7: A poet in Canada named Anne Carson just won the biggest prize in the world for writing. A poet makes pictures out of words, and hers come from old Greek stories, older than the oldest castle. When the phone rang with the news she was in Iceland, listening to the wind. She was surprised and very happy.

At 11: A committee in Sweden picks one writer a year for the Nobel Prize in Literature, the biggest prize in books. This year it is Anne Carson, a Canadian poet who taught ancient Greek for 30 years and writes poems that work like novels. Like science fair judges, they score the whole table, not one project.

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can hold two true things at once: she made new forms, and she spent 30 years inside the oldest ones.
