Elon Musk posted on X before 4 a.m. Monday that next year he will give one SpaceX share to "every kid in America." A share closed Friday at $146.03. The US has about 73 million children under 18, so the pledge comes to about $10.66 billion. He gave no detail on how, or on who counts as a kid. He called it "the first of many."

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: A very rich man, Elon Musk, says next year he will give every kid in America one tiny piece of his rocket company. A company is cut into pieces called shares, like a pizza cut into millions of slices. One slice costs about $146 today. He hasn't said how yet, and that's fine.

At 9: Sunday, the AI company Anthropic said it would put 5% of its shares in a public fund. Musk replied: give them straight to the public. By 4 a.m. Monday he had posted his own offer: one SpaceX share, about $146, for each of America's 73 million kids. A coupon with no store named on it.

At 14: Is $10.6 billion a lot for Musk? He holds billions of SpaceX shares, so one each for 73 million kids is a small dent. Second: it came a day after Anthropic put 5% of its shares into a public fund. His reply: "give the shares directly to the public." He has given $276 million to the midterms since July.

Why this works at age 14: A teenager can hold two true things at once: $10.6 billion is a real sum, and it is a small dent for a man who owns billions of shares. The script gives both figures and the timing beside Anthropic's fund, then lets them weigh it. Expect "why does he want to?" The articles don't say. Say so, and ask what they would guess.
