Novaya Gazeta, started in 1993, cleared out its Moscow office Monday. The government had blocked its websites, seized its printing house, cut off reader donations and ordered it out of the building. Six of its journalists have been killed. Its editor says it will return one day. A new sign went up outside: 'Here lived Novaya Gazeta.'

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: In Russia, a newspaper that told people what the government was doing wrong had to stop. The government blocked its websites, took its printing machines and told it to leave its building. It's like being told you can't use the classroom anymore, so the class ends. It happened far away, and the grown-ups are watching it.

At 11: Novaya Gazeta spent 33 years reporting on corruption in Russia. After Putin invaded Ukraine, the government blocked its websites, seized its printing house, cut off donations and ordered it out of its office. No step was a ban. Together they ended the paper. A referee calling a foul on every touch.

At 16: Nobody banned Novaya Gazeta. The government blocked its sites, took its press, cut off its money and evicted it. Is that different from a ban, or just slower? Six of its journalists were killed and it kept going. Second: the editor says it will return once the country stirs. Do you believe him?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script hands over one paper told to stop and a classroom taken away, which they have felt. Putin and Ukraine never come up. Expect 'can they write it at home?' Some reporters moved abroad in 2022 and started a new paper there. The editor says this one will come back.

The dinner table question: If you can't ban something, is making it impossible the same thing? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
