At age 7: Only citizens vote in America, and every town keeps a list of who can. Today the top judges said the government can use a big computer list to help check those town lists. It's like checking the class list against the school's list: now and then a name is spelled wrong. The grown-ups in each town still do the last check.

At 10: DHS keeps a list of people who aren't citizens. Trump's team made it a tool that checks voter rolls in bulk. A judge stopped it in June: the data had mistakes. Today the Supreme Court let it run, 6-3. It's a spell-checker: it flags, a person decides.

At 16: If a check wrongly flags a few real voters, is it worth running? The majority says the government must answer states asking who's a citizen. Jackson: a few lost lawful voters outweigh waiting. Second: purges stop 90 days before an election. What changed?

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out chains of cause and effect, so the script runs one chain, list to tool to judge to court, and hands them a spell-checker, which flags real words too.
