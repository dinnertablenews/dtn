At age 6: Christiane Amanpour has told people the news on TV for 43 years, longer than most of your teachers have been alive. She flew to places where wars were happening and told everyone far away what she saw. Next year she will stop and try new things. She chose it herself, and she is fine.

At 9: In 1983 one of Amanpour's bosses told her she didn't have what it took to be a foreign correspondent. She became CNN's best-known war reporter anyway, from the Gulf War to Ukraine. At 68 she has set her own leaving date, June 2027. One wrong call, then a scoreboard 43 years long.

At 15: Amanpour's rule is "always be truthful, not neutral." Can a reporter be both, or does one give? Second: she gave no reason, and the news came two weeks after an $81 billion merger gave CNN a new owner. Coincidence, or the reason she didn't say?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out whether adults judge fairly, so the script hands them a chain to check: one boss said no, the record said otherwise for 43 years. The scoreboard is the mechanism, evidence outlasting an opinion. Expect "why leave if she's so good?" She didn't say. Her boss says it is the right time.
