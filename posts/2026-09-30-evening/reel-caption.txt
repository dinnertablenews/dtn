No crime, says the watchdog. But the bill doubled.

The Fed's inspector general found no grounds to believe Jerome Powell or the Fed broke a federal law on the headquarters renovation, a report said Wednesday. The Justice Department opened a criminal probe of the then chair in January and closed it in April. The report faults the Fed's management: costs more than doubled, to $2.5 billion.

Source: Axios

At age 6: The grown-ups who look after America's money are fixing up their big office. It cost more than twice the plan, like a lemonade stand meant to cost five dollars that cost twelve. Some people thought the boss cheated, so a checker looked. Nobody cheated, they just spent too much, and the grown-ups have it.

At 9: The Fed is fixing up its headquarters, and the cost doubled to $2.5 billion. Trump challenged its chair, Jerome Powell, and the Justice Department opened a criminal case in January. The Fed's own inspector general, a referee inside the building, found no crime. It did find a team that let the price climb.

At 17: If an investigation finds no crime, was it worth running? Prosecutors opened a criminal case into Jerome Powell in January. Wednesday the Fed's watchdog said no law was broken. Powell called it pressure over rates. Second: the Fed still let the cost double. Is no crime the same as no problem?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out cause and effect and whether grown-ups apply rules the same way every time, so the script hands them the chain: the cost doubled, the president challenged Powell, prosecutors opened a case, the referee checked. The verdict splits in two, no crime and bad management, the fairness call they can chew on. Expect "so who pays?" The articles don't say.

The dinner table question: You went way over budget but broke no rule. Are you in trouble? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
