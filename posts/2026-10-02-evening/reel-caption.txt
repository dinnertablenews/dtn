At age 6: The government started building a tall steel fence through a park in Texas, next to a river with cliffs. Today a judge said stop, until a court decides whether the rules were followed. The fence would be as tall as five grown-ups standing on each other's shoulders. The park is still there, and the grown-ups have it.

At 9: Building a wall through a national park means following laws that protect the land. The government waived them to go faster. A park group, a church's friends and a river guide sued, and on Friday a judge stopped the work until the case is decided. A whistle, not the final score.

At 17: Is "remote and rugged" a border defence? Big Bend has the fewest crossings of nine sectors, and the government skipped environmental laws there to build faster. A judge halted the work Friday. Second: the government's lawyers called no witnesses. Why?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out whether adults follow the rules they set, so the script hands them the chain: laws waived, a suit filed, a judge's whistle. The referee is the mechanism, and a whistle is a pause, not a verdict. Expect "so did the government cheat?" The other side will probably win a point, and that is enough to stop play.
