Free sunshine, and no neighbors to say no.

The FCC approved SpaceX's plan to put up to a million solar-powered satellites in low orbit to process data for AI. The project is called Starmind. Starlink, the company's internet network, has more than 10,000. Deployment must start by 2033, with half up by 2038. The first one is due next year; how many will follow is unclear.

Source: BBC News

At age 6: A company called SpaceX got a yes to send up to a million satellites into space to do computer work for AI. They would fly far higher than any airplane and run on sunshine. Down here, those computers fill big buildings that need lots of power and water, and some towns don't want them nearby. Nothing changes for you.

At 9: AI needs huge computer buildings that use so much power and water that towns have started saying no. SpaceX's answer: put them in orbit, where the sun powers them and nobody lives next door. The FCC, the referee for who uses orbit, said yes to up to a million. Starlink has more than 10,000.

At 16: Is it a win when the company with the most satellites gets a licence for a million more? Starlink has more than 10,000; critics say orbit is crowded, Musk says it isn't. Second: the FCC called this a boost for "America's super intelligence economy." Regulator, or cheerleader?

Why this works at age 6: A five-year-old reasons from what they can see, and nobody can see a satellite. So the script puts them above the airplane, the highest thing a child has watched, and runs them on sunshine they already know. A million is a sound at this age; the one going up next year is a number they can hold. Expect 'can we see it?' Not yet.
