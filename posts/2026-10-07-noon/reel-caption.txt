A year of pointing its customers at the nominees.

The Emmys have aired on a broadcast network every year since 1955. From next September they stream on Prime Video, free, no subscription, in a six-year deal between Amazon and the Television Academy. The show once drew more than 20 million viewers. It draws about 7 million now, and this year's lost to Monday Night Football.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: The Emmys are a big party where TV shows win gold statues. It has been on regular TV since 1955, before your grandparents were watching. Next year it moves to Prime Video, like a party moving from the living room to the den. It stays free to watch. Nothing changes for you.

At 11: The Emmys ran on a broadcast network every year since 1955, lately rotating ABC, CBS, Fox and NBC. Fewer people watch: 20 million at the peak, about 7 million now. So the Academy signed six years with Amazon: one home, free, like keeping the same coach instead of starting over.

At 15: Why would a TV awards show give up on TV? The Emmys drew 20 million once, about 7 million now, and this year's lost to a Chiefs-Broncos game. Second: Amazon will spend the year pointing customers at the nominees, then show them the Emmys. Audience, or an ad with trophies?

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the Emmys got a home for six years and stay free, and the home is a store that will push the nominees at its customers all year. The script asks whether that is a bigger audience or an ad with trophies. The article supports both. Expect "who cares?" Seven million, down from 20.
