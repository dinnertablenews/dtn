The Treasury fined Bank of America $1.2 billion Tuesday for failing to catch and report money laundering. The bank let more than $5 billion in suspicious activity go unreported, $1 billion of it tied to national security risks. It admitted it willfully broke the law. It is the third-largest penalty in Treasury history.

At age 6: A big bank called Bank of America has to pay $1.2 billion. Its job was to watch for people hiding stolen or secret money, and it did not watch closely enough. A bank is supposed to be like a lifeguard, eyes on the pool the whole time. This one kept waiting for someone else to shout. The grown-ups have it, and your piggy bank is fine.

At 12: Banks have to watch customers' money and report anything that looks like crime. Regulators say Bank of America waited for police and other banks to point things out, like a smoke detector with the battery out, waiting for the neighbours to smell smoke. It admitted it and pays $1.2 billion.

At 13: If a bank admits it willfully broke the law for years, why is the answer a fine? Bank of America pays $1.2 billion, about 17 cents a share, and reports its earnings the next morning. Second: regulators say it put revenue ahead of compliance and let outside tip-offs do its watching. Who was watching the watcher?

Why this works at age 13: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the bank admits it willfully broke the law, and the cost is 17 cents a share on results day. The script asks whether that is a penalty or a line item, then who checks the checker, since regulators ordered fixes in 2024. Expect "does anyone go to jail?" Not here. It's civil.
