Three orangutan mothers, their three daughters and two adult males went back into a reserve in western Borneo on Friday. Indonesian rescuers pulled 46 of the apes out of wildfires in August and September. El Niño made this year's fires worse. About 104,000 orangutans are left, down from more than 230,000 a century ago.

At age 7: Eight orangutans went back to the forest in Borneo on Friday. Big fires burned the trees where they lived and ate, so rescuers picked them up and kept them safe. One mum and her baby were stuck in a little patch of trees with farms all round, like an island. The new forest has food and water. They are home.

At 12: Borneo gets fires every dry season, and El Niño made this year's worse. The fires burned the forest the orangutans eat from, so they wandered into plantations looking for food. Rescuers picked up 46. On Friday eight went into a reserve picked like a campsite: water, food, a way out.

At 17: Is it still wild if people chose the forest? Eight orangutans went into a Borneo reserve on Friday, picked for its food, water and a link to other forest. Second: El Niño made the fires worse, and cleared land burns faster. Which can anyone fix?

Why this works at age 12: This age is learning cause and effect, so the script hands them one chain: a dry season, an El Niño year, forest burned, apes in the plantations, a rescue, a reserve. The campsite is the rescuers' checklist: food, water, a way out. Expect "why not put them back where they lived?" It burned; that is why they moved.
