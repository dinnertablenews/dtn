At age 7: A movie company picked a new actor to play James Bond, a pretend spy. Five actors tried out in the real costumes on the real movie sets, like dressing up to try out for the school play. They chose Robert Pattinson. That is the whole story: an actor got a job.

At 10: Amazon makes the Bond films now, and its founder, Jeff Bezos, says he helped pick the new one. Five actors did screen tests in full costume on real sets, like a tryout where everyone runs the same drill. The panel nearly chose someone less famous. Then, Bezos said, it was obvious.

At 17: Should the man who owns the studio pick the actor? Jeff Bezos says he was "heavily involved" in casting Robert Pattinson as Bond, the first under Amazon. Five finalists, and a panel that first leaned away from him as too famous. Second: should fame count for him, or against?

Why this works at age 10: At ten a child is working out rules and whether adults apply them fairly. The script hands over one chain: five finalists, the same test, a panel that leaned against the famous one, a pick. That is a tryout, and they have stood in one. Expect "so did being famous help or hurt?" Bezos says it nearly hurt. Doubting that is fair.
