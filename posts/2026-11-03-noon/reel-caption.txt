At age 6: Today is Election Day. Grown-ups all over the country go to a voting place, mark a paper and put it in a box, like voting for the class pet. Tonight people count every paper to see who gets to make the rules in Washington. More than 57 million grown-ups did theirs before today. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: For two years Congress has said yes to most of what Trump asked. Today voters pick who sits there. Democrats need three more House seats and four in the Senate to take over, like tug of war where one step wins. Then the other side can call the hearings.

At 16: Is a midterm a vote on the president? Trump told a Kansas crowd Sunday to pretend he is on the ballot, and 88% of Republican TV ads ran where he won in 2024. Second: a president's party almost always loses seats at the midterm. If it happens tonight, what does it prove?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two true things at once: Trump asked for this to be a vote on him, and the president's party loses seats at most midterms. The script hands them both and asks what tonight proves. Expect "will he accept it if he loses?" He hasn't said so, and courts have turned back his lawsuits.

The dinner table question: If your team loses, is it the captain's fault? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
