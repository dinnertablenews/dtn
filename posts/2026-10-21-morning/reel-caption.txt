At age 7: A man named Elon Musk is the boss of two giant companies. One builds electric cars and the other builds rockets. He says he'd like to join them into one company, like two Lego sets snapped together. Lots of other people have to agree first, so nothing has happened yet.

At 9: Elon Musk runs Tesla, the car company, and SpaceX, the rocket company. Tuesday he said merging them would be a good idea, but he can't join the talks because he'd be on both sides of the table. Other directors at each company decide instead, a referee called in because both captains are one person.

At 17: Musk says a Tesla-SpaceX merger would be a good idea, and that as a conflicted party he can't sit in the talks. Can a founder step out of a room he built? Together they'd be worth about $3.5 trillion. SpaceX has lost $1.1 billion this year, and its shares jumped 8% on the news. What is the market buying?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out rules and whether adults follow them, and this story is a rule in action: the man who wants the deal stepped out of the talks. The committee is the referee, and the shareholder vote is the second check. Expect "why can't he just decide?" The companies belong to their shareholders, and he is one of many.
