At age 6: On Monday the planes at New York's airports mostly stayed on the ground all day. The people who guide planes talk to them through a long wire, and it broke. The spare wire had already been cut by workers digging near the train tracks, like losing your key on the day the spare is gone too. By Tuesday it was fixed and the planes were flying again.

At 12: The controllers who guide planes into New York get radar and radio through one main line. On Monday morning it failed. The backup should have taken over, but railroad workers had already cut it, so Newark took no landings from 9:40 to 5:20. The spare tire you find is flat the day you get the puncture.

At 15: Who takes the blame when a backup fails: the crew that cut the cable, or the system that never checked it? The transport secretary blamed Amtrak. Amtrak blamed New Jersey Transit. Second: how many wires is enough?

Why this works at age 6: A five-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, and the feature could be planes in trouble. The script gives them a broken wire and a missing spare key, and puts the planes where they were, on the ground. Expect "can our plane break like that?" The plane was fine. A wire on the ground broke, and it is fixed.
