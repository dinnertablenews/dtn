Fewer than 5,000 votes out of nearly a million.

Democrat Scott Colom beat Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi by fewer than 5,000 votes out of nearly a million. He is the first Black senator from the state in more than 140 years, and the first Democrat it has sent since 1982. Hyde-Smith conceded Wednesday in a video. Democrats will hold 55 Senate seats, with Alaska still to be called.

Source: BBC News

At age 6: Mississippi has a new senator, Scott Colom. Nearly a million people voted, and he won by fewer than 5,000. If 200 kids voted on a game, that is winning by one kid. He is the first Black senator from Mississippi in more than 140 years. The grown-ups have it.

At 8: Scott Colom drove to all 82 Mississippi counties and ran on prices, calling his opponent "Cindy High Prices" over Trump's tariffs. He won by fewer than 5,000 votes, the first Democrat there since 1982. That close, you'd want the ref to check the replay. Mississippi has no recount law, so she conceded.

At 16: Trump won Mississippi by 22 points. Two years on, it sent a Democrat to the Senate by fewer than 5,000 votes. What flipped it: prices, or a candidate who went to all 82 counties against one who rarely showed up? Second: no recount law, so she conceded fast. Strength, or no option?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two true things: Mississippi went for Trump by 22 points and then elected a Democrat.
