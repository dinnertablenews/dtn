Ping Ping and Fu Shuang left Chengdu early Sunday on a Boeing 777 FedEx calls the Panda Express and landed in Atlanta that morning. Both were born in 2020. Zoo Atlanta holds them on a 10-year loan, its first pandas since 2024. Xi Jinping announced the loan Thursday at his summit with Trump. The pair sits a month in quarantine first.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: Two pandas flew from China to Atlanta this morning on a big plane with no other passengers. Ping Ping has dark circles round his eyes and Fu Shuang has a little bump on her forehead, so you can tell them apart. They get a quiet month in their new house first, the way a new puppy hides under the bed for a week. Then the zoo opens the door.

At 11: China does not give pandas away. It lends them when it wants to be friendly and lets the deal run out when it is annoyed. Thursday, after three days of talks with Trump that fixed none of the big arguments, Xi announced this loan. A library book: Atlanta gets it for 10 years, and it still belongs to the library.

At 14: Three days of Trump and Xi fixed none of the disputes between them. The pandas were one of the few concrete results. Is a 10-year bear loan a real result or a photo op? Second: China has let panda deals lapse when relations sour. If the bears can go home when Beijing is unhappy, who is the loan for?

Why this works at age 11: This age is working out how rules and deals fit together, so the script hands them the chain: China lends, never gives; a friendly summit produced a loan; a sour one can end it. The library book is the mechanism, borrowed and still owned. Expect "so they're not really ours?" No. Ten years, then home, sooner if the governments fall out.
