At age 7: A golfer from South Korea named Tom Kim won a gold medal in Japan. In his country every young man has to spend about a year and a half in the army, longer than a whole school year. Win a gold medal and the country says you can do a few weeks instead. When his last putt dropped he let out a roar.

At 10: Every man in South Korea serves 18 to 21 months in the army before he turns 28. Win gold at the Asian Games, or any Olympic medal, and you do only basic training, under a month. Tom Kim, 24, missed at the Paris Olympics. This time he shot 64 and won by three. A hall pass you earn by beating everyone.

At 15: Should a gold medal get you out of the army? South Korea says yes. Tom Kim, 24, won the Asian Games by three shots, and his 18 to 21 months of service becomes basic training, under a month. Second: teammate SH Kim, 28, took silver in the team event. Silver gets nothing. Fair, or exactly the point?

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can hold two true things at once: a country may reward the people who win for it, and the line between gold and silver falls on one man with no second chance at 28.
