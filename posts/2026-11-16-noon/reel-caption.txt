At age 6: A prince from England named Harry flew to Ukraine, a country at war, to visit soldiers who got hurt. He watched them play rugby in wheelchairs, crashing into each other like bumper cars. Then they all cheered his name and crowded in for selfies. He has left Ukraine now, and the grown-ups have it.

At 9: Ukraine has more than 150,000 wounded soldiers. Prince Harry runs the Invictus Games, sports for wounded troops, and 400 Ukrainians are trying out for next year's. His trip stayed secret until he'd left, the way a surprise party only works if nobody knows.

At 13: Does a famous visitor help a country at war, or mostly himself? Prince Harry got a huge welcome in Lviv and told wounded veterans "you're going to win the war". Second: Ukraine's doctors now teach the world about trauma, he said. Who wants that expertise?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid picture, so the script hands them wheelchairs crashing like bumper cars, not the war that put the men in them. What frightens a child this age is what they can see, so the slide shows a cheering gym and says the prince has left. Expect "did the soldiers get better?" Some walk again. Some won't.
