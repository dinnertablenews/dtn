At age 7: Some men in England were planning to hurt a place where big American planes park, like a parking lot for airplanes, and the police caught them first. Nothing got hurt, not one plane and not one person. The planes have flown all the way home across the ocean. The grown-ups have it.

At 10: US bombers flew from RAF Fairford in England to hit Iran. On Sept. 27 police found gasoline in three vans and arrested seven men. Now Britain's prime minister says Iran's Revolutionary Guard directed it. Nobody is charged yet: the referee saw the foul and is still checking the replay.

At 15: Britain sanctioned two Iranians on Monday over a plot it says Iran's Guard directed, and has charged nobody. Can a government be sure enough for one and not the other? Second: Iran says a base that bombs it is a target, and Britain let the bombers fly. Did Britain pick a side?

Why this works at age 10: This age is learning rules, so the script runs the chain: bombers strike Iran, vans with gasoline at their base, seven arrests, a prime minister naming Iran. The referee checking the replay is the gap between sure and proven, one every kid has argued. Expect "why aren't they in jail?" On bail, while police build the case.
