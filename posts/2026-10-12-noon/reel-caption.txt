At age 6: A missile hit an airport in Saudi Arabia, a country so far away it takes a whole day to fly there. Some people were hurt and some died. The airport is closed while grown-ups work out how it happened. You are safe here, and the grown-ups have it.

At 8: The Houthis, a group in Yemen at war with Saudi Arabia, fired drones and missiles at the airport in Riyadh. They flew more than 600 miles and got past the defenses built to stop them. Thirteen people died. Now the Saudis go to the replay, like a referee, to see what failed.

At 16: Why does a country with Patriot missiles let one through? Drones went in first, then missiles that change course in the air, and the Saudis are low on interceptors. Thirteen people died at Riyadh's airport on Saturday. Second: Trump says he may send US strikes. Should he?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the defenses were there, and they failed. The script gives the probe's sequence, drones, then missiles, then interceptors running low, before the harder question, whether the US should join. Expect "will we go to war?" Trump says he is looking at it. Nothing is decided.

The dinner table question: If the shield fails once, do you fix the shield or hit back? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
