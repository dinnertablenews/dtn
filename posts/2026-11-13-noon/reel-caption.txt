At age 6: A building in New York fell down on Wednesday, into a big hole where workers were digging next door. Some people died. Firefighters got there in one minute, about the time it takes to tie your shoes, and worked all night getting people out. Buildings almost never fall. Our house is fine, and the grown-ups have it.

At 8: On Wednesday a 1930s apartment building in Brooklyn collapsed into the construction pit next door. Eight people died, two of them children. Nobody knows yet whether the digging caused it, so the city is checking 100 other sites by the same developer, like a referee stopping play to look at the replay.

At 16: Should the city check 100 other sites because one building fell? New York is, after a 1930s block in Flatbush collapsed into the pit next door and eight people died. Nobody has said why. Second: the DA is already talking charges. Justice moving fast, or a verdict before the evidence?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, and here it is a building falling. The script hands them a different one: firefighters there in one minute, working all night. The comparison is a shoe-tie, not a hole, because this age fears what it can picture.
