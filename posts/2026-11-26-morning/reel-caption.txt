Macy's postponed its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday, with the balloons already being inflated. At age 7: The giant balloon parade in New York isn't on today. A big windstorm is coming, and a balloon as big as a house could get blown into a lamppost. So Macy's moved the parade to Saturday, and TV shows last year's today. The balloons are folded up in a warehouse, waiting.

At 9: The parade has a wind rule, like a lifeguard's flag at the pool: balloons stay down if gusts pass 34 mph. It came after 1997, when wind pushed a balloon into a lamppost and badly hurt a woman. Thursday's forecast says gusts to 60. So the 100th parade moves to Saturday, the first postponement ever.

At 14: Why not fly the balloons lower, like 2019? The rule grounds them above 34 mph gusts, and the forecast says 60, so there was no middle setting. Second: the balloons were already going up when Macy's called it. Caution, or hoping the forecast would change?

Why this works at age 14: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the wind rule left no room, and Macy's waited until the balloons were going up to say so. The script hands them 2019, 34 and 60, then asks about timing, the question the adults were asking too. Expect 'would they have flown if NBC had nothing to show?' I don't know. Nobody has said.
