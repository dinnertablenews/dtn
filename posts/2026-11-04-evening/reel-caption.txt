At age 6: Mike Johnson is the Speaker, the person in charge of the House, where a lot of our laws get made. His team lost the big vote on Tuesday, so he says he won't be the boss of his team anymore. It's like a captain handing over the armband after a hard loss. He still has his job, and the grown-ups have it.

At 8: The party with the most House seats picks the Speaker. Republicans lost that majority on Tuesday, so in January the gavel goes to the Democrats, likely Hakeem Jeffries. Johnson could have run to lead the smaller Republican side; he told his staff he won't. Lose enough games and the captaincy gets rechosen.

At 15: In October Johnson told CBS he'd stay on whatever happened. After the loss he told staff he won't run. Can a leader change his mind when the numbers change, or was October a campaign line? Second: Axios says Republicans are watching whether Trump backs a successor. Should he get a say?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script gives them a captain handing over the armband after a loss, one picture for a shuffle of jobs. They also read your face before your words, and a level voice tells them this is ordinary. Expect "is he sad?" Probably, and he still has a job. Then on.
