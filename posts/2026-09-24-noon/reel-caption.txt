At age 6: Every week the government counts how many grown-ups lost their job and asked for help while they find a new one. Last week the count was the smallest since summer. Picture a packed stadium where a few people leave early: that's how few. Most grown-ups' jobs are steady right now.

At 10: When someone loses a job, they file a claim for unemployment checks. So the weekly count works like a smoke detector for layoffs: 197,000 last week, the fewest since mid-July. Bosses remember the hiring shortage after the pandemic, so they keep staff. Hiring is slow, though.

At 16: Is a job market where nobody gets fired but few get hired a good one? Claims hit 197,000 last week, the fewest since mid-July, but employers add 80,000 jobs a month, under half the 2023 pace. Second: bosses keep staff because the post-pandemic shortage scared them. Is fear a sound reason?

Why this works at age 6: A five-year-old reads your face before your words, and "unemployment" on the radio sounds like a threat to the house. The script turns the count into a stadium where a few people leave early, one picture for the whole report, and ends on the line they keep: most jobs are steady. Expect "could you lose your job?" Say what is true.
