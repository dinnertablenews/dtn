At age 7: Russia and Ukraine have been at war for four and a half years, and lately they hit each other's power plants, the big machines that make electricity for a city's lights. On Sunday our president said both sides agreed to stop. Ukraine said it would if Russia did. Russia kept going that night, and the grown-ups are still talking.

At 11: Trump wants Ukraine to stop hitting Russian oil refineries; he says the strikes push up US diesel prices. Sunday he posted that both sides had agreed to stop. Ukraine said yes if Russia does. Russia said nothing, then hit a Kharkiv power plant. A ceasefire is a handshake: it takes two hands.

At 14: Can a ceasefire exist because another country's president posts that it does? Trump wrote Sunday: "Do not break it!" Ukraine said yes if Russia did; Russia said nothing. Second: the White House calls it "refinery for refinery." Ukraine has one refinery. Russia has a vast network. Fair trade?

Why this works at age 7: A child this age takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script hands them power plants and the lights they run, and leaves out the six dead. They read your face before your words, so say it flat. Expect "does that mean the war is over?" No. Russia hasn't answered, and the grown-ups are still talking.
