Bosnia does. One each for three peoples.

Bosnia elects three presidents at once, one Bosniak, one Serb and one Croat, a system built by a US-brokered peace deal in 1995. Milorad Dodik, a pro-Russia Serb nationalist banned from office last year, declared victory for his party on Sunday. His ally led the Serb seat by about 30 points. Turnout was 42%, down around 8% from 2022.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: People in a country called Bosnia voted today, and they pick three presidents at once, one for each of its three big groups. Imagine a team with three captains who have to agree before anyone moves. Tonight the grown-ups there are counting the votes.

At 10: After a war, a peace deal split Bosnia's top job three ways so no one group runs the country alone. Think three referees who all have to blow the whistle. Milorad Dodik was banned from office for defying the deal's overseer, so his party ran an ally. She won by 30 points.

At 15: If a court bans a politician and his party wins anyway, did the ban work? Milorad Dodik was off Bosnia's ballot for the first time in nearly 30 years and still declared victory, crediting Trump and Putin. Second: turnout fell to 42%. When people stop voting, who decides?

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out rules and whether adults apply them evenly, so the script hands them the chain: a war, a peace deal, a job split three ways, a man banned for defying its overseer, and his ally winning anyway. Expect "so did the ban do anything?" Honest answer: it kept his name off the ballot and nothing else.

The dinner table question: Could you run a team with two co-captains you disagree with? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
