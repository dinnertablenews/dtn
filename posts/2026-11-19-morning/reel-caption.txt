Buyers bet on what it earns later. A bet can lose.

Anthropic priced its stock at $83 a share on Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 it had planned. That values the maker of the Claude AI tool at $1.5 trillion and raises $81 billion, more than SpaceX did in June. The shares trade on the Nasdaq from Thursday under the ticker ANT. The company owes $6.2 billion and is not profitable.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: A company that makes a computer helper called Claude let people buy pieces of it today. Think of a pizza cut into 980 million slices, with every slice costing 83 dollars. People bought all of them, and the company will use the money to build bigger computers. Nothing changes for you.

At 8: Anthropic makes the Claude AI tool. Building AI takes giant computers, and it has pledged $900 billion for them. So on Wednesday it sold pieces of itself, called shares: 980 million at $83 each, for $81 billion. Like a pizza sold by the slice to pay for the oven.

At 17: Is $1.5 trillion a bet or a bubble? Buyers paid $83 a share for what they think Anthropic earns later, after it spends $900 billion on computers. Second: it promises to weigh "the long-term benefit of humanity" against profit. Who checks that?

Why this works at age 17: A seventeen-year-old can hold two true things: buyers paid 83 dollars a share, and the company loses money. The script gives them both, then asks who checks a promise to weigh humanity against profit. Expect 'so is it a bubble?' The report says some investors fear one. Nobody knows until it pops or doesn't.
