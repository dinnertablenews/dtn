The short road was mined. They went the long way round.

Evacuees from Russian-held Oleshky crossed into Ukraine on Saturday, by bus and plane through Russia and Belarus. A ceasefire brokered by Melania Trump got them out; it runs through Sunday. Relatives waited at the border with aid tents and pet food. Liza Paliy, 19, last saw her father 2½ years ago. Ukraine won't say how many came.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: Some families in Ukraine got their dads back today. They had been stuck in a city called Oleshky, and the short road out had land mines, so buses took them the long way round through two other countries. Think of your longest car trip; this one was longer. At the end, daughters waited at the border, and the grown-ups had food ready.

At 10: Russia took Oleshky early in the war, and the roads out were mined, so about 2,000 people were stuck with little food. This week both sides paused the shooting there, like a timeout in a game, and the road got cleared. Buses took people out through Russia and Belarus, and on Saturday they crossed home.

At 16: Would you shake hands with the enemy to get people out? Ukraine's human rights chief rode into Oleshky beside his Russian counterpart to run this, and some Ukrainians hated the sight. Second: Ukraine won't say how many came home, to protect those who stayed. Is secrecy part of a rescue?

Why this works at age 7: A child this age reasons from what is in front of them, so the script hands them one picture: a long bus trip home, and a daughter at the border. The war, the occupation and the ombudsman are not in it. Expect "did everyone get out?" Several hundred chose to stay, and food is being brought in to them.

The dinner table question: Would you work with someone you hate to get people home? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
