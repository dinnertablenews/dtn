At age 6: A shark as long as a car swam into a canal in a city in South Korea and couldn't find her way back out. She stayed a whole month, and people gave her a name, Bukangi. Big crowds came to the edge to look at her. Helpers used long nets to steer her back to the sea, and now she is out.

At 10: Last month a three-metre shark turned up in a canal in Busan, South Korea. The water was unusually warm and full of fish, so she may have followed dinner in and stayed. Nets laid out like cones steering traffic pointed her back at the sea. At least three tries, huge crowds, and now she is out.

At 15: Why did it take a month to get her out? Officials in Busan tried nets at least three times to guide a three-metre shark back to the sea while crowds lined the canal. Second: the BBC says unusually warm water may have drawn her in. If the sea keeps warming, is this a one-off or the first of many?

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can reason about what is not in front of them, so the script hands them the one detail that points past the canal: the water was unusually warm. They can hold the rescue and its cause together and decide whether the cause worries them. Expect "is that climate change?" The BBC doesn't say, and the question is fair.
