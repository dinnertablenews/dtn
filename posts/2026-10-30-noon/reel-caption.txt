Reuters says a new job: running the World Economic Forum.

The European Central Bank said Wednesday that its president, Christine Lagarde, will step down in February, eight months before her term ends. The rumour had run since January. Reuters reports she may go on to lead the World Economic Forum. EU officials want her successor picked before France votes for a president in April.

Source: Semafor

At age 7: Christine Lagarde, who runs the bank that looks after Europe's money, said on Wednesday she will leave her job in February, sooner than planned. Her bank keeps prices steady, the way a thermostat keeps a room from getting too hot. Nothing changes for your money today. The grown-ups there are picking who comes next.

At 12: Christine Lagarde runs the European Central Bank, which sets the cost of borrowing across Europe, like a thermostat for prices. On Wednesday it said she leaves in February, eight months early. Prices are rising, Semafor says, so leaders want her successor chosen before France votes in April.

At 13: If you ran Europe's central bank, would you leave eight months early for a bigger job? Reuters says Lagarde may go on to lead the World Economic Forum. Second: EU officials want her successor picked before France's April election, which the far right is expected to win. Prudent, or a way around voters?

Why this works at age 13: A teenager can hold two true things at once: a leader may leave early for a better job, and the people replacing her may be hurrying to beat an election. The script asks whether that hurry is prudence or a dodge, and the argument improves once they take a side. Expect "who picks the next one?" EU leaders, before April.
