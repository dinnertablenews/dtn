Karl Bushby left his mother's house in Hull, England, in 1998 to walk around the world. On Monday he walked back in. He set out from Punta Arenas, Chile, crossed four continents and 30 countries, walked the Bering Strait ice and swam the English Channel rather than take a boat. He is 57. His mother asked, "Where have you been?"

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: A man named Karl walked all the way around the world and got home to his mum on Monday. He left when he was 29 and he is 57 now, and he walked 31,000 miles, more than once around the whole Earth. When the sea was in the way he swam, or walked over the ice. His mum had a banner up that said "Welcome Home Son."

At 8: Karl Bushby made himself one rule in 1998: every mile home on foot, like a board game where you can't skip a square. That rule is why it took 28 years: Bering Strait ice, a decade of Russian visa trouble and a crash that took his sponsors' money. On Monday he walked into Hull.

At 16: Would you give 28 years to one promise? Karl Bushby said in 1998 he would walk every mile home or not come home, and on Monday, at 57, he did. He barely saw his son grow up. Second: he says it took a world of strangers to get him there. Whose win is it?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script gives them one picture: a man walking, and a mum at the gate with a banner. The miles mean nothing until they become a lap of the Earth. Expect "did he miss his mum?" Yes. He hadn't seen her in ten years, and she cried.

The dinner table question: What promise would you keep for 28 years? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
