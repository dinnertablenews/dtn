At age 7: In California, 548 people were in prison waiting to be put to death for terrible crimes. The governor said no one will be put to death. They stay in prison for the rest of their lives, behind a door that stays locked for good. The grown-ups have it.

At 12: California had 548 people sentenced to death, but it hasn't executed anyone since 2006. Governor Newsom changed every sentence to life in prison, no parole. He says the system has convicted innocent people. For nearly 400 the state's top court must agree: a second referee checking the call.

At 17: California's voters kept the death penalty in 2012 and 2016. Newsom just commuted all 548 sentences. Can one official overrule the voters? Second: he says an imperfect system can't be trusted to kill. Is life without parole harsher anyway?

Why this works at age 12: A 12-year-old is working out rules and who applies them, so the script hands them the chain: a death sentence, no executions since 2006, a governor changing every one, a top court signing off on most. The second referee is the check. Expect "what if one didn't do it?" His argument: it has got it wrong before.
