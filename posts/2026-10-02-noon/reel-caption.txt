Hardly anyone is hiring, and hardly anyone is firing.

US employers added 29,000 jobs in September, down from 133,000 in August. Economists had expected 90,000. Unemployment rose to 4.2%. Wages grew 3% in a year, the smallest gain since 2021. Few are being laid off and few hired, so the jobless now wait more than six months on average. It is the last jobs report before the November 3 election.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: Every month the government counts how many new jobs there are. This month it counted 29,000, which is small for a country this big, like one scoop of sand on a whole beach. Most grown-ups kept the jobs they already have. The grown-ups have it.

At 11: The government counts jobs every month. September added 29,000; August added 133,000. Companies aren't firing many people, but they aren't hiring either, so anyone who loses a job now waits six months to find one. Musical chairs, with no chairs added and none taken away.

At 15: Who does a slow month hurt most, the people with jobs or the people looking? Wages rose 3% in a year, the smallest since 2021; the jobless now average six months out of work. Second: it's the last jobs report before November 3. Does one month's number deserve that weight?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, and 29,000 is not. The scoop of sand on the beach is the anchor: small against big, something they can picture. Vygotsky's point is that the words go to the adult, so the script ends on the grown-ups having it. Expect "will you lose your job?" Answer straight; most people kept theirs.

The dinner table question: Would you leave a safe job to hunt for a better one? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
