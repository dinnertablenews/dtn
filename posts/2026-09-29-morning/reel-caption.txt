So every school plays by one rulebook.

The Protect College Sports Act cleared the Senate on Monday, 77 to 22. It lets the NCAA set and enforce one national rulebook on transfers, recruiting and paying players. A school can share about $50 million a year with athletes, who get five years of eligibility and one free transfer. The House left for the election on September 16.

Source: Axios

At age 7: The people who make our country's laws voted last night on rules for college sports, the games on TV on Saturdays. The rules say how players can switch teams and how much a college can pay them. It's like every school agreeing on one rulebook before kickoff instead of each bringing its own. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: College players can now earn money and switch schools through a transfer portal. The Senate voted 77 to 22 for one national rulebook: the NCAA enforces it, players get five years and one free transfer. One referee, one rulebook. The House has to vote too, and it went home for the election.

At 15: Should Congress write the rules for a game? The Senate just did, 77 to 22: one national rulebook, a cap on player pay, one free transfer. Second: Ted Cruz wants the House back for a day so Trump can sign it on the College GameDay set before the election. Governing, or campaigning?

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the bill can be a real fix for college sports and a signing on the College GameDay set can be an election ad. The script gives them both and lets them argue which. Expect "why is Cruz so keen?" He told Semafor that voters who ignore politics care deeply about college sports.
