At age 6: Yesterday grown-ups all over the country voted, and last night the counting finished. Trump's team lost a lot of seats in Congress, the big building where the country's rules get made. Now the other team has more chairs, like when the other end of the seesaw gets heavier. Trump is still president, and the grown-ups have it.

At 11: Congress is the gate a president's plans go through, and for two years it mostly waved Trump on. On Tuesday voters handed Democrats the House and the Senate, even in states Trump won easily. Now they hold the gate: they can vote his plans down and call hearings, and he says he will veto theirs.

At 15: Trump asked voters to pretend he was on the ballot, then told Fox News it was not a personal defeat: voters "didn't like the candidates." Can both be true? Second: Democrats hold both chambers now, and Trump has posted a "veto" cap. Two years of blocking, or a reason to deal?

Why this works at age 11: This age is working out rules and whether adults apply them, so the script hands them a gate and who holds it: a vote, a majority, and what the majority gets to do. Veto is a new word and a good one, a no that beats a yes. Expect "so who's in charge now?" Both sides, which is the point, and it means slower.

The dinner table question: If you lose a vote, do you have to go along with it? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
