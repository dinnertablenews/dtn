At age 7: In Alaska, two men named Dan Sullivan want the same job, and both names are on the voting paper. The president's lawyers asked a judge to pull out one Dan's votes and check them, like scooping marbles out of a jar before the count. On Sunday the judge said no. Votes are secret. At 13: Should a court set aside ballots to check what voters meant? The DOJ asked in Alaska, and Judge Sharon Gleason said no: "no workable plan" that kept ballots secret, and days too late. Second: the DOJ says she never ruled on whether the second Dan is a decoy. Does that soften the loss?

Why this works at age 9: This age is testing whether adults apply rules evenly, and the script hands them the chain whole: a lawsuit, a judge, a no, with the reason attached. Secret ballots are a rule they can check, and a referee who won't change the rules after kickoff is a call they make at recess. Expect "so did the DOJ lose?" Say yes, for now.
