Not the rain. The wind, and 70,000 already without power.

A nor'easter hit the Northeast on Saturday. Streets flooded in Surf City and Manasquan, New Jersey, and New York and New Jersey declared emergencies. Forecasters called for 65 mph gusts on Cape Cod and 20-foot waves. Flights were cancelled in Boston, ferries stopped, the Red Sox moved a game, and Ed Sheeran called off two stadium shows.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: A big storm is blowing along the top of the country tonight. In some beach towns in New Jersey the sea came up the streets, deep enough to float a paddleboard. Up in Boston the wind is so strong they cancelled a concert. The weather people saw it coming, so people stayed home, and it passes by the end of the weekend.

At 12: A nor'easter spins up the Atlantic coast and shoves the sea onto land. Saturday's full moon lifted the tide higher. Then a chain: gale winds knock down trees, trees take down power lines, 70,000 customers go dark. Gillette Stadium called off Ed Sheeran for safety.

At 14: Would you have cancelled Ed Sheeran, or waited for the wind? Gillette Stadium said safety and pulled two nights. Second: Jersey Shore mayors asked people on low streets to leave. Nobody was ordered. When is leaving mandatory, and who pays if they stay?

Why this works at age 12: This age is working out chains of cause and effect, so the script hands them one to run: wind to trees to power lines to dark houses, with a full moon lifting the tide to make it a system. Expect "why didn't they just wait for it to pass?" They did. The Red Sox moved Sunday's game, and the island ferries stopped until the water calms.

The dinner table question: What's the one event you'd still show up for in a storm? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
