At age 6: Far away, in a country called Saudi Arabia, a drone, a flying machine with nobody inside, hit an airport and some people died. That is on the other side of the world from us. People there watch the sky all night, and they stopped four more drones that same night. The grown-ups have it.

At 10: Yemen's Houthis and Saudi Arabia kept a truce for four years. In July it broke. Now Saudi jets bomb Yemen and the Houthis send drones at Saudi airports. On Wednesday one hit Dammam's airport and killed two; 21 have died at airports this month. A playground fight where each says the other hit first.

At 14: Why hit an airport, not an army? Payback for Saudi air strikes on Yemen, the Houthis say; 21 people have died at Saudi airports this month. Second: Yemen's own count is at least 1,574 dead in three months and 256,000 driven from home. Why does the airport make the news and the village not?

Why this works at age 14: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the airport attack is the headline, and the village count is the bigger number. The script hands them both and asks why one makes the news. Kuhn's point is that arguing is a skill you practise, so let them. Expect "should Saudi Arabia stop bombing then?" I don't know. The Houthis say so.
