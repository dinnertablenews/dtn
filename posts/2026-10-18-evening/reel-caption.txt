Pope Leo XIV appeared at his window over St Peter's Square on Sunday, four days after surgeons removed the lower lobe of his right lung. The crowd cheered. He thanked them for their prayers and said he will gradually take up his duties again. The tumour was adenocarcinoma. His surgeon says the November trip to South America can go ahead.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: The Pope, who leads the Catholic church, had an operation on Tuesday to take out a sick bit of his lung. On Sunday he came to his window and waved, and the crowd below cheered like a stadium. His doctors say he will be mended in about ten more days. He is being looked after by good people.

At 12: A week ago the Pope told the crowd doctors had found a lump on his lung. Tuesday surgeons took it out, with part of the lung. It was cancer. Sunday he was back at the window, thanking them. A lab now tests the lump, like a mechanic reading a pulled part, to decide on more treatment.

At 14: Should a leader announce his illness a week before surgery, then show up four days after? The Pope did both, from the same window. Second: his surgeon has cleared a 12-flight trip to South America in November while the lab still tests the tumour. Confidence, or pressure to look well?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script hands them the wave and the stadium cheer, and leaves the word cancer out unless they bring it. Cantor's work says an ill leader frightens this age less than a parent's worried face. Expect "could you get sick like that?" Anyone can. Most people never get this one.
