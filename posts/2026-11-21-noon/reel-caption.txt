A federal jury in Los Angeles convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, of lighting a fire on Jan. 1, 2025 that hid in tree roots for six days, then flared into the Palisades Fire. At age 6: Two winters ago a man lit a fire on a hill near Los Angeles, and the wind spread it until thousands of houses burned. Twelve grown-ups on a jury listened for days and decided he did it. A fire like that starts smaller than a birthday candle. He will be punished, and that fire is long out.

At 12: On New Year's Day 2025 someone lit a fire on a hill above Pacific Palisades. It kept burning deep in the tree roots, and six days later the wind brought it back. It burned 6,800 buildings and killed 12 people. The jury worked a detective's board: his car at the trail, his phone on the hill, no fireworks on camera.

At 13: Why did the second jury convict when the first voted 10-2 to acquit? Prosecutors dropped the ‘revenge on the rich’ story and stuck to his car at the trailhead, his phone on the hill, and cameras that caught no fireworks. The defense: hikers crossed the spot for six days first. Is that reasonable doubt?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes the one vivid piece, fire, and puts it at the bedroom window, so the script says far away and long out before anything else. The birthday candle makes the start small enough to picture. Cantor found this age fears what it can see. Expect ‘could it happen here?’ Say firefighters are close, and fire is a grown-up job.
