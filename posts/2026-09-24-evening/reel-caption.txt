The Iran war. Their trip home kept getting pushed back.

Eight sailors with the USS Abraham Lincoln attempted suicide on a deployment that kept the carrier at sea a record 260-plus days for the Iran war, acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand. None died. The ship carried a psychologist and a therapy dog, Cao says. Gillibrand calls the conditions "serious and deteriorating."

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: Some sailors on a giant ship have been away from home since last November, about as long as a whole school year, and a few of them got so sad they tried to hurt themselves. Nobody died. The ship has doctors on it and even a therapy dog, and it is sailing home now. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: The Lincoln left San Diego in November, due home in May. The Iran war kept pushing that back, until it hit a record 260 days at sea. A deployment is a countdown, and every time the end moved, the crew started over. Eight sailors got so low they tried to end their lives. None died.

At 14: Eight suicide attempts among 6,000 sailors over 10 months: a scandal, or, as one psychiatrist says, actually low? Second: Trump called nine months at sea "a good period of time." Gillibrand calls the conditions "serious and deteriorating." What would settle it?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out cause and effect, so the script runs the chain: a ship leaves, the war moves the return date, the crew starts the countdown again. The countdown is a mechanism they run themselves, and it shows how a delay becomes a weight. Expect "why didn't they just come home?" Sailors go where they are ordered.
