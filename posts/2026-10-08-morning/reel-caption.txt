At age 7: The president has a weekend place in the woods called Camp David. Now he says presidents should also get to stay at his golf club in Florida, two minutes from the airport, and his club will pay for it. Camp David is cabins hidden in a park, like a secret fort you can't see from the road. The grown-ups are sorting out whether that's fair.

At 10: Camp David is a hidden Navy camp of cabins in a Maryland park. Trump says presidents should also get weekends at his golf club in Florida, and his club will build it as a gift. Like a kid offering to host every sleepover at his house: generous, and the house is still his.

At 15: Is it a gift if the giver keeps the house? Trump's club offered to build a presidential retreat at "no cost to the federal government" and give future presidents free membership. He says "this wouldn't be for me." Second: Camp David is a hidden Navy camp. Is a golf club as safe?

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out fairness, so the script hands them the chain: a camp the Navy runs, a club the president owns, a letter offering it free, a gift that stays his. The sleepover host is a mechanism they own. Expect "is that allowed?" Nobody in the articles says it isn't. A county board got the letter.
