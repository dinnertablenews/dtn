At age 7: Thieves on the internet got into a big company called Binance on Thursday and took money out, like a hand slipping into a till when nobody is looking. It was more than six billion dollars, the most ever taken that way. Nobody was hurt. Binance says it will pay everyone back, and the police are hunting the thieves.

At 9: Binance holds people's digital money like a bank holds dollars. On Thursday someone got into one engineer's laptop, Binance says, and pulled out $6.3bn, the most ever in one hack. Other crypto firms froze half before it was spent, like a bank blocking a stolen card. Binance says it will cover every loss.

At 15: If a company is robbed of $6.3bn and pays everyone back, was anyone hurt? Binance says it will cover all losses; a trader told Axios a smaller exchange would have collapsed. Second: two analytics firms blame a North Korean hacking unit. If a state runs heists for income, is it crime or policy?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out chains of cause and effect, so the script hands them one: a laptop is broken into, the laptop can move money, the money goes, other companies freeze half of it.
