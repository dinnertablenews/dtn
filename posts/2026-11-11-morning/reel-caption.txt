Nobody else ran. 'Who would want the job?'

Senate Republicans reelected John Thune of South Dakota as their leader Tuesday, a week after an election that cost them at least seven seats and the majority. He ran unopposed in a secret ballot. Ron Johnson and Josh Hawley wanted a delay to think it over. The conference voted to proceed, and both walked out. Trump stayed out of it.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: The Republicans in the Senate lost a lot of seats in last week's election, so they are the smaller team now. On Tuesday they voted to keep the same captain, John Thune from South Dakota. Some wanted a timeout to think first, like a team that lost asking for a minute. Nothing changes at our house.

At 11: Republicans lost at least seven Senate seats, so in January they lose the majority. Before that, each party picks its leaders. Some senators wanted a delay, like calling a timeout before a big call. The group voted to go ahead, and John Thune, running alone, kept the job.

At 13: Lose seven seats and keep the same boss? Senate Republicans did on Tuesday: John Thune ran unopposed and won. Johnson and Hawley asked for a delay. Hawley asked what rushing it says. Second: if nobody else wants the job, is that a reason to keep someone, or a problem on its own?

Why this works at age 13: A teenager can hold two true things at once: Thune won every vote cast, and his party lost at least seven seats under him. The script asks whether an empty ballot is confidence or a shrug, and at 13 they are testing whether you will argue it honestly. Expect 'what did he do wrong?' Colleagues say the loss wasn't his.
