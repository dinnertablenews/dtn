A base has walls and soldiers. His house was online.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has left his Georgetown home for a military base after Iran-linked threats, US media reported Wednesday. A source told CBS the move followed a Wall Street Journal photo that let people locate his house. Bessent runs the sanctions campaign against Iran. Hegseth and Rubio already live on bases.

Source: BBC News

At age 6: A man called Scott Bessent has a big job: he looks after America's money. Some people far away said they wanted to hurt him, so he moved out of his house and onto an army base, where soldiers guard the gate like a castle wall. He is safe there. The grown-ups whose job this is are watching, and it is nothing to do with our house.

At 10: Scott Bessent runs the US Treasury, and since August his job has been cutting Iran off from the world's banks. US intelligence says Iran is going after American officials. Then a newspaper ran a photo of his house, and a photo of a front door gives away a street. So he moved onto a base.

At 13: Should a paper print a photo showing where a cabinet secretary lives? The Journal did, readers found Bessent's house, and now he lives on a base. Second: Bolton and Milley are on Iran's list too, reportedly, and lost their guards last year. Protection for the office, or for the man?

Why this works at age 10: At 10 they follow chains of cause and effect, so the script hands over the whole one: a job that squeezes Iran's banks, a warning from US intelligence, a photo in a newspaper, a move to a base. The photo is the link they can test: a front door gives away a street. Expect 'can he just leave?' Yes. The government moved him.
