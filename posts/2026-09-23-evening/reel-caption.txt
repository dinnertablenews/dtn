The FBI is the country's top police detectives, and the thieves say they copied the names and home addresses of the people who work there, like copying every page of a class phone list. The FBI is checking what was really taken. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: In May the FBI called the hackers ShinyHunters a criminal gang. The group says it hit back: it broke in and copied files on 38,000 agents and applicants. It says it wants no money, only the warning gone, and gave the FBI a week. The FBI is checking every door, like a locksmith after a break-in.

At 16: If a gang says it hacked you to punish a warning about it, do you take the warning down? ShinyHunters gave the FBI a week and says it wants no money. Second: BBC News says a sample looks real; the FBI can't yet say where the hole is. Does that matter to the 38,000?

Why this works at age 9: This age wants the chain, and this one runs clean: the FBI wrote a warning, the group says it broke in to answer it, and now it wants the warning gone. Fairness is their subject, so the demand is the part to argue: does a thief get to edit the police notice? Expect "did they really do it?" The FBI says it cannot tell yet.
