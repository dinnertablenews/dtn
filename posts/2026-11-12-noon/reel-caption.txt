Fish need homes too. It's a reef now, 180 feet down.

The SS United States crossed to Europe in three days in 1952 and still holds the record. It is longer than the Titanic. After nearly 30 years at a Philadelphia pier, a Florida county bought it, cut holes in the decks and opened the valves on Wednesday. No explosives. It now sits 180 feet down off Destin, the world's largest artificial reef.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: A huge old ship called the SS United States was sunk on purpose in Florida on Wednesday. It is longer than the Titanic, and nobody was on board. Underwater it becomes a reef, a place where fish can live. Divers will go down to visit it.

At 8: The SS United States crossed to Europe in three days in 1952 and still holds the record. Then jets came, and it lost money. It sat at a Philadelphia pier for nearly 30 years. A Florida county cut holes in its decks and opened the valves: no explosions, like pulling the plug on a bath.

At 15: Is a ship that carried Truman, Kennedy and Coco Chanel worth more as a museum or as a reef? The group that fought to save it lost; its president, whose grandfather helped design it, says she is glad the story lives on. Second: a county paid millions to sink it for divers. Keeping it, or burying it?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, and the vivid feature here is a sinking ship. So the script says the thing they will worry about first: nobody was on board, and the grown-ups planned it. The Titanic is the measure because it is the one ship they know. Expect 'do the fish like it?' Yes, that is the point.
