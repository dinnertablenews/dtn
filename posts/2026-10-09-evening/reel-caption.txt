At age 7: The president made a deal with Russia today to get diesel, the fuel that runs big trucks and tractors. Diesel costs about double what it did when the year started, so the trucks that bring food to the store cost more to fill. Russia attacked its neighbour Ukraine, so a lot of grown-ups are angry about buying from it. That argument is theirs to have, and nothing changes for you tonight.

At 11: Diesel hit a record $6.52 in September, double since the Iran war began. Farmers and truckers run on it, and voters pick a Congress on November 3. So Trump called Putin: 300,000 tons now, more later, three weeks after a law to squeeze Russia's fuel money. A thermostat turned the other way.

At 15: Who pays for cheaper fuel? Three weeks ago Trump signed a law to choke Russia's energy money. Friday he agreed to take its diesel, 25 days before an election. Zelensky says Russia repays "with terror and perfidy." Second: if a price drops because a war runs longer, is that a saving?

Why this works at age 7: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script puts diesel in the trucks that bring food to the store and the tractors on a farm, then says why it costs more. One fact about Russia, no sanctions, no Putin. Expect "is Russia bad?" It attacked Ukraine, and the president is buying from it anyway. Say both.

The dinner table question: Would you buy from someone you'd punished if it saved you money? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
