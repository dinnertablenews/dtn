At age 7: A town in Colorado called Boulder says two big oil companies knew their fuel was heating the planet and hid it. Boulder wants them to help pay for fires and floods. On Monday the top judges in the country listened to both sides, like a teacher hearing two kids before deciding. They will decide by summer.

At 10: Boulder, Colorado, sued Exxon and Suncor in 2018, saying they deceived people about climate change and should pay for fires and floods. The companies say only Washington can rule on pollution. The Supreme Court heard both sides, like a referee ruling on whether a game counts at all.

At 14: Can one town bill Exxon for climate change? Exxon's lawyer says 90,000 towns could then set energy policy by jury. Boulder's says it wants damages for deception. Second: Alito, who owns oil stock, sat out. With eight justices, a tie lets Boulder's case go ahead.

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out rules and who gets to apply them, and the case is exactly that: whether a town may even ask a jury, before anyone asks what Exxon did. The referee ruling on whether the game counts hands them the distinction. Expect "so did they lie or not?" The court is not deciding that. If Boulder wins, a Colorado jury does.
