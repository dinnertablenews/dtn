Microsoft says its own upgrade broke it. Not hackers.

Microsoft's Outlook and Teams stopped working Thursday afternoon and stayed mostly down for nearly 24 hours, in more than 70 countries. Hospitals, schools, courts and the US House lost their email. Microsoft blames a network upgrade that went wrong, not hackers. It said sorry Friday and has no date for a full fix.

Source: BBC News

At age 7: A company called Microsoft runs the email lots of grown-ups use at work. On Thursday it stopped, all over the world, for almost a whole day, like a mailbox with the slot taped shut. Nobody did it on purpose. Some schools used paper instead. Most of it works again now, and the grown-ups have it.

At 12: Microsoft was upgrading its network Thursday, and the upgrade broke Outlook and Teams for more than 430 million people, for nearly a day. Police in Britain said phone, not online forms. One mistake reached that far because so many people use one company, like a town drinking from one well.

At 13: If one company's mistake can take email from hospitals, courts and the US House in 70 countries for a day, is that company too big? Microsoft's own upgrade did it. Second: Matt Saunders of Adaptavist told the BBC a second system is almost impossible in practice. Then what is the plan?

Why this works at age 12: At twelve they are building cause-and-effect chains, and this one is clean: an upgrade, a break, a day of phone calls instead. The well is the mechanism, one source for the whole town, and it says why a mistake in one place reached 70 countries. Expect "was it hackers?" Microsoft says no, its own upgrade.
