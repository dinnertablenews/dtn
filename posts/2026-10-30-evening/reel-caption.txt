Yes. He was born in Chicago. He gets a ballot like anyone.

The Vatican confirmed Friday that Leo XIV, born in Chicago, submitted a mail-in ballot for the November 3 midterms. He is registered in Will County, Illinois, and asked for the ballot on October 3, ten days before his lung surgery. The Vatican says he has voted whenever eligible, in Peru too, and that citizens have a duty to take part.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: The pope leads the Catholic church, and he grew up near Chicago. This week he filled in his ballot, the paper grown-ups vote on, and mailed it home to Illinois like a birthday card to Grandma. No pope had ever voted in an American election. His counts the same as anybody's.

At 11: Becoming pope did not cancel Robert Prevost's US citizenship. Americans abroad can vote by mail, so on October 3 he asked Will County, Illinois for a ballot and sent it back. His vote is sealed like everyone's: records show which primaries he picked, never who he chose.

At 15: Should another country's head of state vote in ours? Leo XIV is a US citizen, and the law lets citizens abroad vote by mail, even if they work for a foreign government. He has clashed with Trump. Can a vote nobody sees take a side?

Why this works at age 6: A five-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script hands over one object, a paper mailed home, and skips the Vatican. The birthday card does the work a definition can't. Expect "Did he vote for the good guy?" Say nobody gets to see, and that is the rule for everyone, popes included.
