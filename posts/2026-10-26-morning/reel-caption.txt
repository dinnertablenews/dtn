Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: María Corina Machado went home to Venezuela on Sunday after ten months away. The people running her country wouldn't let her run against them, so she hid, then left on a boat at night. She walked into a church and everyone there hugged her, like a whole team when the bus gets back. She's home, and her friends are with her.

At 10: Machado won her side's primary in 2023 with over 90% of the vote. The government barred her, so her ally González ran, and credible evidence shows he won 2-to-1, the AP says. The ruling party's vote council named Maduro the winner anyway. A spelling bee judged by one kid's dad.

At 13: Is Venezuela freer than a year ago? Maduro is in a New York jail, and Machado walked into a church to chants of "Freedom!" But the acting president the US backed got 90 days, has stayed past them and called no election. Who gets to say when the next one is held?

Why this works at age 10: This age is working out rules and whether the adults keep them, so the script hands over the chain in order: she won, got barred, her stand-in won, the count said otherwise. The judge who is one kid's dad is the mechanism. Expect "why didn't anyone stop them?" The US did, in January. Then it picked the next president itself.

The dinner table question: If the referee is on the other team, do you still play the game? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
