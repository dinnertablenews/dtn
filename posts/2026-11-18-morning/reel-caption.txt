Prince William handed out his Earthshot Prize in Mumbai on Tuesday: £1m, about $1.3 million, to each of five winners. Bhutan won for policies that guard water for 1.6 billion people across South Asia. Ørsted won for cutting its direct emissions 98%, coal to offshore wind. Mahindra won for electric rickshaws. William rode in one.

Source: BBC News

At age 6: A prince from England flew to India and gave out five prizes for taking care of the Earth. One went to a whole country, Bhutan, for keeping its rivers clean for the people downstream. One went to a company that makes little electric taxis with three wheels, about the size of a golf cart. The prince rode in one and said it was really good fun.

At 12: The Earthshot Prize has five goals: clean air, clean oceans, less waste, fix the climate, protect nature. Fifteen finalists, five winners, £1m each to grow the idea. Ørsted ran on coal, switched to offshore wind and cut its emissions 98%. It's a science fair where the prize money builds the project bigger.

At 13: Can a whole country win a prize? Bhutan just did, for water policy that reaches 1.6 billion people. Second: Ørsted won for cutting more than 98% of its direct emissions, and it got there by quitting coal. Does a company earn a climate prize for cleaning up what it burned?

Why this works at age 13: A thirteen-year-old can hold two true things at once: Ørsted burned coal, and Ørsted cut 98% of its emissions. The script asks them to weigh the past against the fix, the argument the judges already had. Kuhn calls that a practised skill; this is a case. Expect "will a prize fix anything?" Not alone. £1m each is seed money.
