Radio moves at light speed. It still needed 24 hours.

Voyager 1 is one light-day from Earth, 16 billion miles out. Its call reached Earth on Thursday exactly 24 hours after the probe sent it, which is how long radio takes to cross that distance. The probe launched in 1977 and left the sun's bubble in 2012. It is 49 years old and still runs on plutonium. Its twin gets there in 2035.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: A spaceship called Voyager 1 left Earth almost 50 years ago. It is so far away now that when it calls home, the call takes a whole day to get here. If it yelled hello, you'd hear it tomorrow. It carries a gold record of Earth sounds, in case anyone out there finds it. It is still going.

At 11: Radio travels at the speed of light, the fastest anything can go. Voyager 1 is so far out that even at that speed, its call took 24 hours to reach Earth. One light-day is the distance light covers in a day, 16 billion miles. Voyager flies 38,000 mph, so it needed 49 years to get there.

At 15: Voyager 1 hasn't sent science data since Saturday. The carrier signal still comes, so NASA knows where it is, not what it sees. Keep paying to listen to a 49-year-old probe? Second: it carries a record Carl Sagan built to last a billion years. Who was that message really for?

Why this works at age 11: At eleven a child is working out cause and effect, and this chain is clean: light has a top speed, the probe is far away, so even the fastest thing we have takes a day to arrive. The 38,000 mph against 49 years lets them check it. Expect 'why not build a faster one?' Nothing goes faster than light. That is the ceiling.
