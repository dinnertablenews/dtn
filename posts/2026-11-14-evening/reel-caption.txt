At age 7: A giant rocket called Starship went around the Earth 14 times and came home on Saturday. Instead of landing on legs, it lowered itself down and two huge arms on its launch tower caught it, like a catcher's mitt the size of a building. Nobody was inside. It's the first time anyone has caught a spaceship coming back from space.

At 10: Rockets used to be thrown away after one flight, like buying a new car for every trip. SpaceX wants Starship to fly again, so on Saturday the ship lowered itself onto its tower and two arms closed on it, the first catch of a ship back from orbit. The booster blew up Friday. Engineers are working out why.

At 17: Does a catch count as a win when the booster blew up on the way? SpaceX caught a Starship back from orbit for the first time; the booster is in pieces in the Gulf. Second: NASA is paying billions for this ship to land astronauts on the moon. How much failure should a moon program accept?

Why this works at age 10: At ten the chain is the point: rockets were thrown away, SpaceX wants this one reused, so it has to come back whole, so the tower catches it. The new car for every trip is the mechanism, and it says why a catch is worth the risk. Expect "why did the booster blow up?" Its engines quit too soon. Nobody knows why yet.
