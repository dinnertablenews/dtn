At age 6: Starting today, some things made in Canada, like three-wheel motorcycles and some milk products, aren't allowed into our country. Canada is our neighbor to the north, and the two countries swap goods every day. It's like two neighbors who each shut their gate after an argument. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: Trump put a 50% tariff on about $20 billion of Canadian goods this summer. Canada hit back, dollar for dollar. Today Trump banned a list of Canadian products: alcohol, some dairy, motorcycles. Like a shoving match: each shove answers the last, and nobody says stop.

At 13: Is it fair to punish someone for hitting back? Trump taxed Canadian goods at 50%, Canada matched it, and today Trump banned $1 billion of Canadian goods for retaliating. Second: Carney says there is now a price for access to the US market. Keep hitting back, or walk away?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script gives them one: a gate between two neighbors, shut after an argument. Tariffs stay out; a gate is a thing they have seen in a yard. Expect "are we still friends with Canada?" Yes. The two countries still trade $880 billion a year, and this ban is a sliver.
