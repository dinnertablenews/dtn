The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, the database police across the US use to check warrants, fugitives, missing persons and stolen property. A source confirmed it to AP on Saturday. The FBI declined to comment, and how far the hackers got is not known. Trump told Fox News: "It's not good."

At age 6: Police all over the country share one big computer list of people they are looking for and things that got stolen. Someone sneaked into that list without permission, like a kid peeking in the teacher's grade book. The FBI, the top police, is finding out who did it and what they took. Nobody is hurt, and the grown-ups have it.

At 11: Every US police department checks one shared list, the NCIC: wanted people, missing people, stolen cars. Someone apparently broke in, and the FBI is working out how far they got. Like a school's attendance file, the copying isn't the danger. It's what a stranger does with the names.

At 14: Who checks the police's own security? The FBI runs the database every cop leans on, someone got in, and it won't say what was taken. Trump called it "not good," then said it might expose the "Russia hoax." Second: when the victim is the government, who decides what the public hears?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, and no computer is. So the script turns the NCIC into a list, and the hack into peeking at the grade book, a wrong they already judge. Expect 'did they take my name?' No: it is a list of grown-ups the police are looking for, and the FBI is checking it.
