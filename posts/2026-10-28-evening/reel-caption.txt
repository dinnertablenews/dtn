The price of borrowing money. The Fed left it where it was.

The Federal Reserve kept its rate at 3.75% to 4% on Wednesday, less than a week before the midterms. It raised the rate in September for the first time in three years. Ten of twelve voted to hold; Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack wanted a quarter point more. Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation is still too high. The next vote is December 8 and 9.

Source: BBC News

At age 6: Today twelve grown-ups in Washington decided how much it costs to borrow money, the way a shop decides what it charges to rent you a bike for an hour. They said: keep the price the same. Ten of them wanted that, and two wanted it a little higher. Nothing changes at our house, and the grown-ups have it.

At 12: Prices rose 3.7% in the year to September, up from 2.4% before the Iran war pushed up gas. The Fed's one tool is the interest rate, a thermostat. Turn it up and borrowing costs more, so people spend less and prices cool. It went up in September. Wednesday it stayed put, to see if the room is cooling.

At 13: Should the Fed raise rates a week before an election? The White House warned against it. Wednesday it held, 10 to 2. Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role. Second: Trump is trying to fire Lisa Cook, who voted to raise. Is the Fed independent if the president can fire its members?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script turns an interest rate into a bike rental, a price for borrowing something for a while. The word belongs to the adult for now; the child keeps the picture. Expect "do we borrow money?" Yes, most families do, for the house or the car, and the price stayed the same today.
