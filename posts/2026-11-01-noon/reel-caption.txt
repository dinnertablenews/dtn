At age 7: A sickness called Ebola is making people very ill in Congo, a country in Africa, across the whole ocean from us. It only spreads by touching a sick person's blood or sick, so the doctors there are very careful. It is not here, and the grown-ups have it.

At 8: Since May, Ebola has infected 10,351 people in Congo and killed 5,026, nearly half. There's no vaccine for this strain, so stopping it means finding everyone a sick person touched, like a contact list. Eight in ten new cases come from people nobody was tracking.

At 16: Would you keep working an Ebola ward after four months without pay? Staff at a clinic in Ituri walked out Friday with five patients inside. Second: a nurse was killed last week by locals who said health workers spread the virus. When half the sick die and nobody trusts the clinic, who walks in?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two things at once: the strikers have a case, and walking out leaves five patients with nobody. The second question is harder, because the people attacking health workers are frightened too. Kuhn calls arguing a skill you practise, so let them run it. Expect "would you stay?" Answer honestly.
