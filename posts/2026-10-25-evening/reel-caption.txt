At age 7: In a country called Serbia, far across the ocean, university students spent two years asking the government to fix things. Today the whole country voted, and the students' side won. The man who had been in charge for 14 years said out loud that he lost. People danced in the streets and set off fireworks, like the Fourth of July.

At 9: In 2024 a concrete canopy fell at a train station in Novi Sad and killed 16 people. Students blamed corruption in the repairs, marched for two years, then put up their own list for Sunday's vote. Vucic's party projects them at 135 to 140 of 250 seats. Like a class vote: pass half and you set the rules.

At 13: Would you trust a strongman who concedes 90 minutes after the polls close? Vucic did, then blamed independent media, European countries and an "American deep state." The Students' List put no student on its ballot and campaigned with no leader. Can a movement with no leader govern?

Why this works at age 7: A seven-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script gives them the fireworks and the man saying out loud that he lost. The canopy and the 16 dead stay in the Try answer; Cantor found this age fears what it can picture. Expect "can that happen here?" Yes. We vote too, and the loser says so.

The dinner table question: If you lost a vote fair and square, would you say so out loud? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
