At age 6: In 1974, a long time ago, a man in Utah killed a teenage girl named Melissa, and the police could not prove who. They kept a tiny piece of evidence safe for 52 years, longer than most grown-ups have been alive. This week a test on it showed it was him. He died a long time ago, so he can't hurt anyone, and the grown-ups have it.

At 9: In 1974 a hunter found Melissa Smith, 17, dead in the Utah woods. The case went cold, but police kept the evidence. In April, DNA tied Ted Bundy to another Utah girl, Laura Aime. Tuesday they said Melissa's evidence matches too, like a puzzle piece you can only place once the one beside it is down.

At 16: Why solve it if he's already dead? Ted Bundy was executed in 1989, and on Tuesday Utah said DNA ties him to Melissa Smith, 17, killed in 1974, so her family finally has an answer. Second: Utah counted 444 unsolved deaths in April. Who decides which get a second look?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two true things: nobody can be punished, and the answer still matters to a family that waited 52 years. The script hands them that pair, then the 444, so the second question is about choosing, which is where they test whether you argue honestly. Expect "why him and not the others?"
