Flávio Bolsonaro lost Brazil's election Sunday by 4.4 million votes. On Monday Brazil's Supreme Court, investigating him for money laundering he denies, ordered him to hand in his passports by noon Tuesday and stay. By Tuesday, multiple outlets reported he had left for the United States. The State Department says he and his family will get visas.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: A man named Flávio Bolsonaro wanted to be president of Brazil, a big country far to the south of us. He lost the vote. A judge told him to hand in his passport and stay, like being kept in at recess, but reports say he left for America instead, and America says it will let him in. Nobody is hurt, and the grown-ups have it.

At 11: Flávio Bolsonaro lost Brazil's election by 4.4 million votes. A court investigating him for money laundering ordered him to hand in his passports and stay. Reports say he left for the US, and the State Department says his family gets visas. A court order is like a fence: it stops at the border.

At 14: Should a country take in a man whose own court told him to stay? Brazil's court told Flávio Bolsonaro to hand in his passports in a money-laundering case he denies; reports say he left for the US, and the State Department says he gets visas. Second: persecuted, or a family dodging its courts?

Why this works at age 11: This age is working out whether rules reach everyone, so the script runs the chain: a loss, a court order, a flight, a visa. The fence is the mechanism, and it stops at the border. Expect "so does he get away with it?" For now, in the US, yes. The investigation in Brazil is still open.

The dinner table question: If a judge says stay and you can leave, should you? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
