At age 7: A judge named Navi Pillay won the Nobel Peace Prize yesterday. She is 85, and her dad drove a bus in South Africa. She grew up to be a judge for the whole world, like a referee for countries. When she heard, she said it must be a joke. It wasn't, and today is a good day.

At 9: Navi Pillay grew up a bus driver's daughter under apartheid. First non-white woman on South Africa's high court, then a Rwanda genocide judge, then the UN's rights chief. The committee says that got war crimes prosecuted. A referee who writes the fouls down after the game.

At 13: Can a court work when the powerful ignore it? Navi Pillay won the Peace Prize for getting war crimes prosecuted. The same day, the US sanctioned the ICC, where she once sat. Second: Israel calls her Gaza inquiry a lie. Can a prize for law stay neutral?

Why this works at age 9: This age is working out whether rules apply to everyone, and who gets to apply them. So the script climbs her career court by court: a province, a country, a tribunal, the UN. The referee writing fouls down after the game is the mechanism they know. Expect "who makes countries obey?"
