At age 6: A man named Chris Murphy, who helps make laws for our country, was at his son's hockey game when his arm went numb, like when your foot falls asleep. Doctors found he'd had a stroke: blood stopped reaching part of his brain for a bit. He's awake now, and his doctors think he'll get all better. The grown-ups have it.

At 11: Sen. Chris Murphy, 53, felt his arm go numb at his son's hockey game Saturday. That's a warning sign, like a smoke alarm: he got to a hospital fast, doctors found a stroke and moved him to Johns Hopkins, a bigger one. He's awake and alert, and his office expects a full recovery.

At 15: Chris Murphy's office says he'll recover fully. It did not say how bad the stroke was. Is a senator's health his own business, or the voters'? Second: John Fetterman had a stroke in 2022 and now works with live captions on a tablet. Where's the line between recovered enough to serve and not?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script opens on a feeling they have had: a foot gone to sleep.
