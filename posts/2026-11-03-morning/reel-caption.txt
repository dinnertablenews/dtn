Clues in the wreckage. They expect pieces far from the site.

A Learjet 60 flying from Austin to the Bahamas dived into a road in Greenacres, Florida, on Sunday, killing all nine aboard. At 35,000 feet the pilot reported ice and turbulence, then a drop of 20,000 feet a minute. On Monday the NTSB said evidence at the site shows the jet was breaking up before it hit. Weather is a main focus.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: On Sunday a small jet flying over Florida went into a storm, higher up than the tallest mountain, and fell. Everybody on the plane died. It hit a road, and nobody on the ground died. Grown-ups whose whole job is finding out why planes crash are there now, picking up every piece.

At 8: At 35,000 feet the pilot reported ice on the jet and turbulence throwing it 700 feet at a time, then a mayday: dropping 20,000 feet a minute. The NTSB says the wreckage shows the jet broke apart before it hit, so the search widens, like hunting a thrown ball outward from where it landed.

At 13: Have you seen the video? The clip of the Learjet diving into a Florida road is everywhere, and nine people died in it, one a 20-year-old copilot. Second: the NTSB says the jet likely broke up before impact and won't name a cause until it has the pieces. Why is slow right here?

Why this works at age 8: This age is working out chains of cause and effect, so the script hands them one in the order the pilot lived it: ice, turbulence, a climb, a mayday, a breakup. The widening search is the method they want next, because this age wants to know how adults know. Expect "so what caused it?" Nobody knows yet, and that is the point.
