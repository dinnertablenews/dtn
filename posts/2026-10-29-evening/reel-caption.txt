At age 6: An actor called Patrick Stewart died on Wednesday at his home. He was 86, as old as a great-grandpa. On TV he was Captain Picard, who flew a spaceship called the Enterprise, but he was pretending, the way you pretend to be a pirate. He got to do his favourite thing for his whole long life.

At 12: Patrick Stewart grew up in a Yorkshire house with no bathroom and memorised Shakespeare for fun. At 14 he learned Macbeth's speeches. After 14 years on stage he took a space show everyone said would flop. It ran 178 episodes, and when he played Macbeth 50 years on, the lines were still there.

At 16: Can a hard childhood make an actor? Patrick Stewart called the stage "a refuge from real life": his father beat his mother. He later wrote that violence "is always a choice a man makes." Second: he had dementia and nobody outside knew. Is a famous person owed that privacy?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so if it comes up the feature is the pretend captain, a game they already play, and the death is a very old man at home. Cantor found the concrete and the near frighten this age; 86 and far away is neither. Expect "will you die?" Not for a very long time.
