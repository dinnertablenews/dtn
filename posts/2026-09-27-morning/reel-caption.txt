Pope Leo XIV said Mass on Saturday on the Place de la Concorde, the Paris square where Louis XVI was guillotined. The Vatican counted 800,000 people, 200,000 more than had registered. At age 6: The Pope went to Paris this weekend and 800,000 people came out to see him. The night before, 80,000 kids filled the big stadium. Saturday's crowd was ten of those. He rode down the street in his slow glass car and stopped to pat babies on the head, like a giant street party.

At 8: France has a rule: the government stays out of religion. So Macron talked with the Pope for an hour on Friday, then skipped the Mass, while his wife and the prime minister went. Like a referee who shakes hands with both captains but never wears a jersey.

At 16: Why would 800,000 go to a Mass in a country where the churches are emptying? France baptised 186,000 people in 2024, against 513,000 in 1980. Second: Macron skipped the Mass, and four rivals for his job went. Neutral, or careful?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two true things at once, so the script gives them a pair: churches emptying, and 800,000 in one square. Kuhn's point is that argument is a practised skill, so let them argue what the crowd means before you offer the baptism numbers. Expect "were they all Catholic?" No. Some came for the crowd, and said so.
