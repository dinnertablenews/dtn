One Republican. He says the Iran war broke the law.

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican who lost his seat, read eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on the House floor Monday. The main charge: fighting in Iran for more than 90 days without Congress's say-so. The House must vote within two legislative days. Hegseth: 'We're not worried about that.'

Source: BBC News

At age 6: A man in Congress named Thomas Massie wants Pete Hegseth, who runs the military, to lose his job. He says Hegseth kept a war going that Congress never said yes to. This week Congress votes, like a class voting on who stays line leader. The grown-ups have it.

At 9: Congress has to say yes before a war runs past 90 days. Massie says the Iran war passed that mark with no yes, so Hegseth broke the rule and should go. Impeachment is the referee's whistle: the House votes on the charge, then the Senate holds a trial. Only two cabinet members have ever been impeached.

At 15: Massie says he may not have the votes and is doing it anyway, because it is right. Principle, or theater? He calls it a gentler version of what Democrats will do in January, and wants Trump to swap Hegseth out first. Second: if the Senate won't convict, did anything happen?

Why this works at age 15: A teenager can hold two true things at once: the vote will likely fail, and Massie says the aim is to make Trump replace Hegseth before January, so a loss is fine. The script sets principle against theater. Expect 'why does Trump call him a loser?' Massie lost his seat and fought Trump on Iran. Neither settles it.

The dinner table question: Would you pick a fight you expect to lose, if you think it's right? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
