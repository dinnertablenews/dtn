Nobody got enough seats. Now they have to make a deal.

Israel voted Tuesday for the first time since the October 7 attack. Exit polls give Netanyahu's bloc 50 seats, Gadi Eisenkot's opposition 52 to 53, and Arab-led parties 17 to 18. Nobody has the 61 needed to govern. More than 74% of voters had turned out by 8 p.m., the most in decades. Final results come in the next few days.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 7: Israel, a country far away, picked its leaders on Tuesday. More people voted than in a long time, but no side got enough to be in charge. Think of a tug of war where neither team can pull the rope over the line. Now the grown-ups there have to talk until they agree on who leads.

At 11: A leader in Israel needs 61 of 120 seats. Exit polls give Netanyahu's bloc 50, Gadi Eisenkot's 52 or 53, and Arab-led parties 17 or 18. Nobody is over 61, so the parties now bargain, like picking teams when nobody has enough players. Both blocs have refused the Arab parties so far.

At 13: Would you take help from a group you promised never to work with? Exit polls say neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Eisenkot's has 61 seats, and the Arab-led parties, with 17 or 18, could decide it. Both have refused them until now. Second: 74% voted, the most in decades. A win for anyone?

Why this works at age 7: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script turns 120 seats into a rope nobody can pull over the line. The word election stays out; the picture does the work. Expect "who's the boss then?" The old one, until they agree on a new one.
