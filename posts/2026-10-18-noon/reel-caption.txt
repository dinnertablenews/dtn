Billed that way. Two years of marches against Trump.

The No Kings network held hundreds of rallies across the US on Saturday, billed as nonpartisan and aimed at early voting in the November 3 midterms. At the Lincoln Memorial, a few hundred people in caps and coats danced to a band. Signs read "VOTE EARLY" and "HANDS OFF MY VOTE!" The network has marched against Trump for nearly two years.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: Yesterday, in lots of cities, people stood together holding signs that said VOTE EARLY. Voting is how grown-ups pick who runs things, like when your class votes on a game. These people want everyone to vote soon instead of waiting. A band played, and people danced in the cold.

At 10: A group called No Kings held hundreds of rallies on Saturday. Their point: in America the president is not a king, and voting early proves it. The rallies were billed as nonpartisan, but the group has marched against Trump for two years. A club that says everyone is welcome, and has a side.

At 16: Can a rally be nonpartisan when its network has marched against one president for two years? Saturday's rallies said vote early. Second: the Justice Department warned officials who count ballots from suspected noncitizens of prosecution. Who is told to use their power, and who to fear it?

Why this works at age 16: A teenager can hold two true things: the rallies were billed as nonpartisan, and the network has opposed Trump for two years. The script sets that beside the Justice Department's warning to ballot counters and asks who is encouraged and who is warned. Expect "will they take ballots?" Three senators asked for a promise. No answer yet.
