At age 6: The president has a helper who runs his office and decides who gets in to see him, like the person at the front desk at school. Susie Wiles had that job, and she quit this week. On Monday a man named Stephen Miller starts in it. Nothing changes for you.

At 9: The chief of staff is the president's gatekeeper: who gets in, who deals with Congress. Tuesday, Trump's party lost Congress. Thursday night Susie Wiles quit; Axios says Trump blamed her. Friday he gave the gate to Stephen Miller, who wrote the immigration rules a poll says went too far.

At 14: Democrats run Congress from January, and the chief of staff is the president's negotiator with them. Their leader, Hakeem Jeffries, calls Stephen Miller "a dangerous xenophobe and reckless extremist." Can those two make a deal? Second: Miller calls the job a fight. Is a fighter the right hire for deals?

Why this works at age 9: At this age they are working out cause and effect and whether blame lands where it should, so the script hands over the chain: a lost election, a resignation two days later, a replacement who wrote the rules voters just rejected. Expect "was it her fault?" Trump blamed her, Axios says. Let them argue it.
