At age 6: There's a narrow gap of sea near a country called Iran where the big oil ships go through. Iran has been attacking ships there, so they stopped going. Now Iran says it will open the gap in seven days if America does some things first. It's like two kids blocking a door and each one saying you move first. The grown-ups are talking.

At 11: Iran attacked ships in the Strait of Hormuz, and gas prices jumped. The US blockaded Iran. Now Iran offers: in seven days it reopens the strait if the US lifts the blockade, waives oil sanctions and holds a ceasefire. A card trade: neither kid hands over first.

At 17: Would you take a deal from someone who broke the last one? In June the two sides agreed a 60-day version; it collapsed when Iran hit ships again. Second: Trita Parsi says a fast deal helps Trump before the midterms. Should an election clock end a war?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so the script gives them a door with two kids in it, a standoff they have stood in themselves. The gap of sea is the one concrete fact; the blockade, the sanctions and the ceasefire stay with the grown-ups. Expect "who moves first?" Nobody has yet, and that is the whole story.

The dinner table question: Would you make a deal with someone who broke the last one? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
