At age 7: America and Iran have been at war since February. On Tuesday the president gave a speech in New York and said the war can end with a deal, or he can hit Iran very hard. That same day people from both sides sat in a room and talked for three hours, longer than a morning at school. Talking is how wars end, and the grown-ups are doing the talking.

At 11: Trump told the UN on Tuesday he has a choice: a peace deal with Iran or, in his word, annihilate it. Iran's delegates walked out. Hours later his envoys met Iran's foreign minister for three hours. It works like a parent counting to three: the threat is there so nobody finds out what happens at three.

At 14: Can a threat to annihilate a country be a peace move? Trump told the UN on Tuesday that is his choice, and hours later his envoys spent three hours with Iran's foreign minister. Second: he says a deal comes after November's election. So who was the speech for?

Why this works at age 11: This age is working out cause and effect and whether a threat is real. The chain here is threat, walkout, then three hours of talks, and the count to three is a mechanism they know from their own side of it. Expect "what if Iran says no?"
