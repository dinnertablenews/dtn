At age 7: Israel had a big vote last week, and no side got enough seats to win on its own. The judges checked and said the vote was fair. Now the president is asking each group who they'd pick, and one leader gets 28 days to build a big enough team. Like picking teams at recess when nobody has enough players. The grown-ups have it.

At 10: Israel's parliament has 120 seats and you need 61 to govern. Netanyahu's side won 53, Gadi Eisenkot's 48, two Arab-led groups 19, so nobody has it. Netanyahu's camp cried stolen; the commission found no evidence. The president, a referee after a draw, picks who tries for 61.

At 17: Netanyahu lost his majority and said Arab-led parties stole the vote. The commission found no evidence. What should happen to a leader who calls a clean vote stolen? Second: Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats, and all but one Jewish party vowed not to work with them. Position or problem?

Why this works at age 10: This age is checking whether adults keep the rules, so the script gives them numbers they can add: 53, 48 and 19 against the 61 needed. Then the referee: the commission checked the stolen-vote claim and found nothing. Expect "what if nobody gets to 61?" A new election early next year, so the president told the parties to bend.
