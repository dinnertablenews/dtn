At age 7: The grown-ups in Congress made a rule to cut off the money Russia uses for its war. It was supposed to start on Sunday. Last night the president pressed pause on it, like the pause button on a timer. The people who wrote the rule are cross, and they will argue about it when they are back at work.

At 11: Congress passed a law to squeeze Russia's money, 86 to 11 in the Senate, and Trump signed it in September. It was due to start Sunday. He used a line in the law that lets the president waive it: a rulebook page saying the referee can skip any rule. Congress is away until after the election.

At 17: If Congress passes a law 86 to 11 and writes in a clause letting the president waive it, who really decided? Trump used the clause and told Congress only that it was in the national interest. Second: Tillis says it's capitulating to Putin; the White House says it protects peace talks. Which needs proof first?

Why this works at age 7: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them, so a sanctions waiver becomes a pause button, and who may press it is a fight they have at home. Vygotsky's point: the words come from the adult, so the script says "cross" and "back at work" rather than "betrayal." Expect "is he allowed?" Yes. The rule says he can.
