To fight the Islamic State. Iraq says it can do that now.

The Pentagon said Wednesday the last US troops left Irbil, ending a 12-year mission against the Islamic State group. Baghdad and Washington agreed the exit in 2024. Iraq's prime minister called it a new phase of sovereignty. Kurdish officials, hit by 1,000 drone and missile attacks this year, call leaving with no air defense a concern.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: American soldiers went to Iraq, a country far away, to help it fight a violent group. That was 12 years ago, longer than you've been alive. On Wednesday the last ones packed up and left, like a coach going home once the team can run practice on its own. Iraq's own soldiers have the job now, and the grown-ups have it.

At 11: In 2014 Iraq asked the US back to fight the Islamic State, which had grabbed big pieces of the country. By 2017 Iraq called the group beaten, and the troops stayed to stop a comeback. In 2024 both governments set an end date, and Wednesday was the day. Training wheels off, on the date circled.

At 15: Would you leave a job on the agreed date if the customer said the roof still leaks? The last US troops left Iraq on Wednesday, the deadline set in 2024. Kurdish officials counted 1,000 attacks this year and asked who guards them now. Second: militias tied to Iran cheered. Wrong call, or only who gains?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old reasons from what is in front of them and takes one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script gives them the coach going home once the team can run practice alone. Nothing about drones or militias, which they can do nothing with. Expect "were they fighting?" Yes, years ago. Iraq's own soldiers finished it.

The dinner table question: You promised to leave by a date. The job isn't tidy. Do you go? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
