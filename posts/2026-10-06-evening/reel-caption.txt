Their schools are falling apart. They want them fixed.

French high schoolers have boycotted class for two weeks over rundown schools: no desks, no chairs, rats. On Tuesday 256,000 people marched with them, the Interior Ministry says, and police fired tear gas in Paris. Classes stopped at 891 schools. Arrests pass 6,100, mostly minors. A 15-year-old lost a hand to a police stun grenade.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: Big kids in France stopped going to school and marched in the streets, because their classrooms have no chairs and leaking ceilings and they want them fixed. Police used a stinging smoke to push the crowds back, and some kids got hurt. It is far away, across an ocean, and the grown-ups there met tonight to sort it out.

At 11: Students at one high school near Paris walked out in mid-September over rundown classrooms. By Tuesday it was 256,000 people in the streets, 891 schools shut, and tear gas in Paris and Marseille. Over 6,100 arrests, mostly minors. A fire alarm: one school pulled it, the whole country heard.

At 16: Is it fair to arrest a 15-year-old for blocking his own school? France has made over 6,100 arrests since the walkouts began, mostly of minors, and one boy lost a hand when a police grenade went off. Second: 715 officers are hurt too. Who says which side went too far?

Why this works at age 6: Cantor's work on fright says a young child takes one vivid feature for the whole event, here police gassing kids, so a parent does not raise it. If it arrives, the script leads with the reason, broken classrooms, which a six-year-old can picture, and puts an ocean between them and it. Expect "did the kids get in trouble?" Some did.

The dinner table question: Your school is falling apart. Is skipping class a fair way to say so? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
