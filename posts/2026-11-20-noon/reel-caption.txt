At age 6: Fiona the hippo at the Cincinnati Zoo died on Thursday. A knit hat flew into her home, she swallowed it, and it got stuck inside her, the way a washcloth clogs a bathtub drain. Her doctors stayed with her for eleven days and tried everything they knew. Lots of people loved her, and it's okay to be sad.

At 9: A visitor at the Cincinnati Zoo tossed a knit hat in the air. A ceiling fan shot it through a gap, and Fiona swallowed it. A hippo's stomach has several chambers, like a cow's, so the hat hid where a scope couldn't find it. Vets can't cut a grown hippo open, so they waited for it to pass.

At 17: Specialists said surgery wasn't an option: skin two inches thick, and days out of water to heal. So the vets waited for her to pass the hat. Was that the right call, or the only one? Second: zoos have warned for years about objects tossed into habitats. Does one small gap above a window count as negligence?

Why this works at age 6: A six-year-old takes one vivid thing for the whole story, so the script gives them the hat, stuck like a washcloth in a drain. Cantor found this age reads your face before your words, so say 'died' plainly and let the sadness show. Expect 'can they get another Fiona?' No. There are two other hippos. Neither is her.
