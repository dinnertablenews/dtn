At age 6: A company that makes weed spray agreed to pay a lot of money to people who got very sick after using it for years, mostly on farms. The company says the spray didn't do it, but it is paying so the arguing stops. It's like chipping in for a broken window when nobody agrees who threw the ball. Spray is a grown-up job, and the grown-ups have it.

At 12: Roundup has killed weeds since 1974. Some 200,000 people say it gave them a cancer called lymphoma and sued Bayer, its maker. A Missouri judge approved a deal Friday: Bayer pays up to $7.25 billion and the suits end, like a referee stopping the match and setting the score. Bayer never admits a thing.

At 17: If Bayer says Roundup is safe, why pay $7.25 billion? In June the Supreme Court said Bayer can't be sued in state courts for failing to warn, since federal rules don't require a cancer label. That weakened the lawsuits. Second: WHO says probably, the EPA says not likely. Who should the label trust?

Why this works at age 12: A twelve-year-old is sorting rules from outcomes, and a settlement breaks the rule they expect: someone pays, nobody is found guilty. The referee who ends the match is the mechanism, and 1974 to Friday is the chain. Expect ‘so did they do it or not?’ A couple dozen juries split. This deal ends the rest without a verdict.
