At age 6: Israel is a country far away, and in eight days its grown-ups vote for their leader. On Sunday President Trump said he wants the leader they have now, Benjamin Netanyahu, to win again, like cheering for a friend who is running for class captain. Trump doesn't get a vote there. The people of Israel do.

At 8: Netanyahu wanted Trump's backing for months. They had fallen out over Iran; in June Trump called him "crazy." Sunday, nine days out, Trump endorsed him with a condition: pick anyone else and America might not help with Iran. A kid who lends his bike only if his friend is made captain.

At 17: Endorsement or threat? Trump backed Netanyahu Sunday, then wrote that if Israel picks "the wrong Leader," America would face Iran alone. Second: this month he told Time "I'm not involved in politics anymore." He has endorsed in Hungary, Japan and Poland.

Why this works at age 8: This age is checking whether adults keep their own rules, so the script runs the chain: a falling-out, months of silence, an endorsement nine days out, a condition on the end. The bike and the captain are a deal from their own playground. Expect "is that allowed?" Nothing stops it. Fair is theirs to call.

The dinner table question: Should you tell a friend who to pick in their class vote? Tell us what your kid said, and how old they are.
