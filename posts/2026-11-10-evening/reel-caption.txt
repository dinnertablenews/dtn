The losing side still needs a captain. He picks the fights.

Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio beat Steve Scalise 106-73 Tuesday in a secret ballot to lead House Republicans in the minority. Jordan, 62, co-founded the Freedom Caucus and chairs the Judiciary Committee. He told colleagues 'offense, not defense, wins elections.' Trump stayed out of it. Democrats pick their leaders next week.

Source: PBS NewsHour

At age 6: The Republicans in Congress lost a big vote last week, so now they are the smaller team. On Tuesday they picked a new captain, a man named Jim Jordan from Ohio. It's like a soccer team that lost picking who leads the next game. Nothing changes at our house.

At 8: Republicans lost the House last week, and their old leader is stepping aside. On Tuesday they picked a new one by secret ballot, folded papers nobody can trace, like a class president vote. Jim Jordan beat Steve Scalise 106-73. Scalise had been No. 2 for 12 years, and most wanted a change.

At 13: Does the losing side's leader matter? Democrats can pass big votes now without Republican help. Still, 106 of them chose Jim Jordan over Steve Scalise, a fighter over a 12-year insider. Jordan says 'offense, not defense, wins elections.' Second: is picking the loudest fighter how a party learns from a loss?

Why this works at age 8: At eight they are working out rules and whether adults apply them, so the script hands over the secret ballot, a vote by folded paper, and a count they can check: 106 to 73. The class president vote is the mechanism they know. Expect 'why secret?' So nobody gets punished for their pick. Scalise keeps his seat.
