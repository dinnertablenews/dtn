At age 7: In England last night, police stopped three vans driving toward an air base and arrested five men. The police think the men were planning something dangerous, so the army sent a robot to look inside the vans, and the families nearby went to a rec center for a while, like a fire drill. That was across an ocean. The police have them, and the grown-ups have it.

At 12: RAF Fairford is a British base that American bombers fly from to hit Iran. Iran has said a base used to attack it is a fair target. So when a villager reported three strange vans heading for it at 12:45 a.m., armed police were there fast. Like a smoke alarm: someone checks the moment it goes off.

At 17: Iran calls a base used to bomb it a legitimate target. Britain lent Fairford to the US while its own prime minister opposed the war. Who put the 85 households next door in the path? Second: terror suspects can be held 14 days without charge, against 24 hours for anyone else. Fair?

Why this works at age 7: At this age they take one vivid feature for the whole event, so the script picks it: a robot looking inside a van, and a fire drill for the families next door. The bomb is never named. Shield it: a plot is nothing they can act on. If they hear it anyway, expect "could they come here?" No. Across an ocean, and the police have them.
